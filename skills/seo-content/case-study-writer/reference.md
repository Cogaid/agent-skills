# Case Study Writer -- Reference Guide

Detailed reference documentation for writing compelling case studies that demonstrate customer value.

## SCQA+R Framework Deep Dive

### Situation (10-15% of total length)

The Situation section establishes context and relatability. The reader should recognize themselves in the customer's profile.

**What to include:**
- Company name, size, industry, and location
- Relevant background (growth stage, market position, team structure)
- The operational context that makes the challenge relevant
- Why this company is relatable to your target audience

**Writing tips:**
- Keep it brief -- this is setup, not the story
- Use specific details that signal "this company is like us"
- Include a stat or two that conveys scale
- Name the customer's role/title to help the reader identify

**Example opening:**
> TechScale, a 200-person B2B SaaS company based in Austin, processes over 50,000 customer support tickets per month. Their VP of Customer Success, Maria Chen, leads a team of 35 agents across three time zones.

### Challenge (15-20%)

The Challenge section creates tension. It's the "before" that makes the "after" compelling.

**What to include:**
- Specific problems (not vague "inefficiencies")
- Quantified impact (time lost, money wasted, opportunities missed)
- Emotional toll (frustration, stress, uncertainty)
- What they tried that didn't work
- What would happen if they didn't solve this

**Writing tips:**
- Use the customer's own words wherever possible
- Be specific: "12 hours per week manually tagging tickets" beats "too much manual work"
- Show cascading effects: one problem leads to another
- Include a direct quote that captures the frustration

**Questions to draw this out in interviews:**
- "What was the breaking point that made you look for a solution?"
- "How did this affect your team day-to-day?"
- "What would have happened if you hadn't solved this?"

### Quest (10-15%)

The Quest section covers the evaluation and decision-making process. It addresses the reader's implicit question: "Why should I choose this product over alternatives?"

**What to include:**
- How they discovered your product
- What alternatives they considered
- Their evaluation criteria
- Why they chose you (specific differentiators)
- Any initial concerns and how those were addressed

**Writing tips:**
- Be honest about the competitive landscape -- it adds credibility
- Highlight differentiators without bashing competitors
- Address common objections implicitly through the customer's story
- This section doubles as a trust-builder for readers in the evaluation phase

### Action (20-25%)

The Action section shows how the customer implemented and used your product. It's the "how" that makes results credible.

**What to include:**
- Implementation timeline and process
- Specific features or capabilities used
- How the team adopted the solution
- Any customization or configuration
- Support received during onboarding

**Writing tips:**
- Be specific about features but frame them as actions the customer took
- Include timeline details to set realistic expectations
- Mention any challenges during implementation (and how they were overcome)
- Show the product in context, not in isolation

### Results (25-30%)

The Results section is the payoff. Lead with the most impressive metric.

**What to include:**
- Primary metric improvement (headline number)
- Secondary metrics (supporting improvements)
- Financial impact (ROI, cost savings, revenue gained)
- Qualitative improvements (team morale, customer satisfaction, process clarity)
- Timeline to results
- Future plans with your product

**Writing tips:**
- Lead with the single most impressive number
- Use before/after comparisons
- Calculate ROI if the customer can share numbers
- Include at least one qualitative benefit -- numbers alone feel cold
- End with a forward-looking statement

## Interview Techniques

### Preparation

Before the interview:
1. Research the company (website, LinkedIn, news mentions)
2. Review their usage data if available (features used, timeline, metrics)
3. Prepare 12-15 questions but be ready to go off-script
4. Share questions with the interviewee in advance (reduces anxiety, improves quality)
5. Confirm recording permission

### During the Interview

**Building rapport (first 5 minutes):**
- Thank them for their time
- Explain the process and how the case study will be used
- Mention they'll have full approval before publication
- Start with easy questions (tell me about your company, your role)

**Getting good stories (middle 20-30 minutes):**
- Ask open-ended questions: "Walk me through..." / "Tell me about..."
- Follow up with specifics: "Can you give me a number for that?"
- Use the echo technique: repeat their last phrase as a question
- Allow silence -- people fill gaps with gold
- Ask "why" and "how" more than "what"

**Getting quotable quotes (last 10 minutes):**
- "If someone was on the fence about [product], what would you tell them?"
- "What surprised you most about the results?"
- "How would you describe [product] to a colleague?"
- "What's the one thing that changed most for your team?"

### Post-Interview

1. Transcribe the recording within 24 hours
2. Highlight the strongest quotes and metrics
3. Identify the narrative arc (what's the compelling story here?)
4. Draft within 1 week while the conversation is fresh
5. Send draft to customer for review within 2 weeks

## Metrics That Matter

### By Industry

| Industry | Primary Metrics | Secondary Metrics |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| SaaS | ARR growth, churn reduction, NPS | Time to value, feature adoption |
| E-commerce | Revenue growth, AOV, conversion rate | Cart abandonment, repeat purchase |
| Healthcare | Patient outcomes, cost per patient | Wait times, satisfaction scores |
| Finance | ROI, cost savings, compliance rate | Processing time, error reduction |
| Manufacturing | Throughput, defect rate, downtime | Inventory turns, lead time |
| Marketing | Lead generation, CAC, ROAS | Engagement rate, pipeline velocity |

### Presenting Numbers Effectively

**Use relative change when the number is impressive:**
- "Reduced response time by 73%" (better than "from 4.2 hours to 1.1 hours")

**Use absolute numbers when scale matters:**
- "$2.4M in new revenue" (better than "18% revenue increase")

**Use both when possible:**
- "Cut onboarding time from 3 weeks to 2 days (90% reduction)"

**Use time-to-value when speed matters:**
- "Achieved ROI within 6 weeks"

### Formatting Results

Use visual hierarchy to make results scannable:

```
## Results at a Glance

| Metric | Before | After | Impact |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| Response time | 4.2 hours | 1.1 hours | -73% |
| Customer satisfaction | 3.2/5 | 4.7/5 | +47% |
| Monthly revenue | $180K | $312K | +$132K |
```

## Distribution Strategy

### Where to Use Case Studies

| Channel | Format | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Website (case study page) | Full length (800-1500 words) | SEO-optimized, gated or ungated |
| Sales collateral | One-pager PDF | Include with proposals |
| Email nurture | Key stats + link | Mid-funnel trigger |
| Social media | Quote card + metrics | LinkedIn performs best for B2B |
| Webinar/event | Slide deck or video | Customer co-presents |
| Paid ads | Short testimonial + stat | Retargeting high-intent visitors |
| Press/PR | Press release format | For notable brand names |
| Product pages | Embedded testimonial | Near CTA buttons |

### SEO for Case Studies

**URL structure:** `/case-studies/[company-name]-[result]`
**Title tag:** `How [Company] [Achieved Result] | [Your Brand]`
**Meta description:** `[Company] [achieved X result] using [Your Product]. Read the full case study.`

**Keywords to target:**
- "[Your product] case study"
- "[Industry] case study"
- "[Competitor alternative] case study"
- "How to [achieve result]"

## Legal and Approval Checklist

```
Before publishing:
- [ ] Customer has reviewed and approved all content
- [ ] All quoted individuals have given consent
- [ ] Metrics have been verified with the customer
- [ ] Company name and logo usage approved
- [ ] No confidential or proprietary information disclosed
- [ ] Legal/compliance review completed (if required)
- [ ] Written approval received (email or signed form)
- [ ] Approved version is final (no post-approval changes without re-approval)
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Making your product the hero** -- the customer is the hero; your product is the tool
2. **Vague results** -- "improved efficiency" means nothing without numbers
3. **Too much jargon** -- write for the reader, not the engineer
4. **Skipping the challenge** -- without pain, there's no story
5. **No quotes** -- direct quotes add authenticity nothing else can match
6. **Too long** -- most readers want 800-1200 words, not 3000
7. **No CTA** -- every case study should end with a clear next step
8. **Publishing without approval** -- damages trust and can create legal issues
9. **Single-use mentality** -- plan for repurposing from the start
10. **Ignoring SEO** -- case studies are high-value content; optimize them
