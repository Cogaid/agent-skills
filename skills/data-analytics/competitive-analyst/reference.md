# Competitive Analyst - Reference Guide

## Strategic Frameworks Deep Dive

### Porter's Five Forces - Detailed Application

#### How to Score Each Force

| Force | Score 1 (Low) | Score 3 (Medium) | Score 5 (High) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threat of new entrants | High barriers, strong brands, capital-intensive | Moderate barriers, some differentiation | Low barriers, easy entry, low capital |
| Supplier power | Many suppliers, low switching costs | Moderate concentration, some differentiation | Few suppliers, high switching costs |
| Buyer power | Many small buyers, high switching costs | Moderate concentration, some alternatives | Few large buyers, low switching costs |
| Threat of substitutes | No close substitutes | Some alternatives, moderate switching | Many alternatives, easy switching |
| Competitive rivalry | Few competitors, high growth | Moderate competition, moderate growth | Many competitors, slow growth, low differentiation |

#### Strategic Implications by Force Configuration

| Configuration | Strategic Implication |
|---|---|
| High entry barriers + low rivalry | Defend position, invest in moats |
| High buyer power | Differentiate, reduce buyer concentration |
| High supplier power | Diversify suppliers, vertical integration |
| High substitute threat | Innovate, increase switching costs |
| High rivalry | Compete on value, seek niches |

### SWOT to TOWS Matrix

The TOWS matrix converts SWOT findings into actionable strategies:

| | Strengths (S) | Weaknesses (W) |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunities (O)** | **SO Strategies:** Use strengths to capitalize on opportunities | **WO Strategies:** Overcome weaknesses to pursue opportunities |
| **Threats (T)** | **ST Strategies:** Use strengths to mitigate threats | **WT Strategies:** Minimize weaknesses to avoid threats |

### Value Chain Analysis

| Activity | What to Analyze | Competitive Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound logistics | Supply chain, inventory management | Do they have cost advantages from suppliers? |
| Operations | Manufacturing, service delivery | Is their process more efficient? |
| Outbound logistics | Distribution, delivery | Do they reach customers better/faster? |
| Marketing and sales | Branding, pricing, channels | Is their go-to-market more effective? |
| Service | Support, maintenance, returns | Is their customer experience superior? |
| Firm infrastructure | Management, planning, finance | Do they have structural advantages? |
| HR management | Recruiting, training, culture | Can they attract better talent? |
| Technology development | R&D, innovation, patents | Is their technology ahead? |
| Procurement | Purchasing power, vendor management | Do they get better terms? |

### Strategic Group Mapping

Plot competitors on two strategic dimensions to identify clusters:

```
                    High Price
                        |
              Premium   |   Luxury
              Players   |   Brands
                        |
    Broad Scope --------+-------- Narrow Scope
                        |
              Value     |   Niche
              Competitors|  Specialists
                        |
                    Low Price
```

Common dimension pairs:
- Price vs. product breadth
- Quality vs. market segment
- Innovation vs. market share
- Geographic scope vs. specialization

## Data Collection Methods

### Public Sources

| Source | Data Available | Update Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Company website | Products, pricing, messaging, team | Check weekly |
| Press releases | Partnerships, funding, product launches | Monitor daily |
| SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q) | Revenue, expenses, strategy, risks | Quarterly/annual |
| Job postings | Strategic priorities, tech stack, growth areas | Check monthly |
| Patent filings | Innovation direction, IP portfolio | Check quarterly |
| App store listings | Features, ratings, update frequency | Check weekly |
| Social media | Messaging, engagement, customer sentiment | Monitor daily |
| Conference talks | Strategy, roadmap, thought leadership | As available |

### Customer Intelligence

| Source | Data Available | Collection Method |
|---|---|---|
| Win/loss interviews | Why we won or lost deals | Post-decision interviews |
| Customer advisory board | Feature priorities, satisfaction | Quarterly meetings |
| Support tickets | Product complaints, feature requests | Keyword monitoring |
| Review sites (G2, Capterra) | Ratings, pros/cons, comparisons | Monthly monitoring |
| Social media mentions | Sentiment, complaints, praise | Social listening tools |
| Churn interviews | Why customers left, where they went | Exit interviews |

### Competitive Intelligence Ethics

| Acceptable | Not Acceptable |
|---|---|
| Public website analysis | Misrepresenting identity to gather info |
| Published financial data | Accessing proprietary systems |
| Patent and trademark searches | Inducing employees to share trade secrets |
| Industry analyst reports | Corporate espionage |
| Customer feedback (with consent) | Bribery for information |
| Job posting analysis | Hiring solely to extract information |
| Conference attendance | Violating NDAs |

## Battle Card Framework

### One-Page Battle Card Structure

```
BATTLE CARD: [COMPETITOR NAME]
Updated: [DATE]

WHO THEY ARE (1 sentence)
[Brief description of competitor and their primary offering]

THEIR STRENGTHS          | OUR ADVANTAGES
- [Strength 1]           | - [Our advantage 1]
- [Strength 2]           | - [Our advantage 2]
- [Strength 3]           | - [Our advantage 3]

THEIR WEAKNESSES         | LANDMINES (avoid these topics)
- [Weakness 1]           | - [Topic that favors them]
- [Weakness 2]           | - [Topic that favors them]

PRICING
[Their pricing vs ours -- key differences]

WHEN WE WIN: [Common scenarios where we come out ahead]
WHEN WE LOSE: [Common scenarios where they come out ahead]

KILLER QUESTIONS (ask the prospect):
1. "[Question that exposes competitor weakness]"
2. "[Question that highlights our strength]"
3. "[Question about their specific limitation]"

OBJECTION HANDLING:
"They're cheaper" -> [Response]
"They have [feature]" -> [Response]
"They're the market leader" -> [Response]
```

## Market Sizing Framework

### TAM, SAM, SOM

| Metric | Definition | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| TAM (Total Addressable Market) | Total market demand for product category | Industry revenue or (# potential customers x average price) |
| SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market) | Portion of TAM you can serve | TAM filtered by geography, segment, capability |
| SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market) | Realistic near-term capture | SAM x expected market share % |

### Top-Down vs Bottom-Up

| Approach | Method | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Top-down | Start with industry size, filter down | Quick estimate, early-stage |
| Bottom-up | Start with unit economics, scale up | More accurate, later-stage |
| Triangulation | Use both methods, compare | Best practice for validation |
