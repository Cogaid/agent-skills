# Competitive Analyst

Conduct structured competitive analysis using proven strategic frameworks to understand market positioning, identify opportunities, and inform strategic decisions.

## Purpose

This skill applies rigorous analytical frameworks to evaluate competitors across product, pricing, market positioning, and strategic dimensions. It produces actionable intelligence briefs that support product strategy, sales enablement, and executive decision-making.

## Quick Reference

### Competitive Analysis Framework

| Dimension          | What to Analyze                           | Data Sources                        |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Product            | Features, UX, quality, roadmap            | Product demos, reviews, docs        |
| Pricing            | Models, tiers, discounts, TCO             | Pricing pages, sales intel          |
| Market Position    | Segment, ICP, messaging, brand            | Website, press, analyst reports     |
| Go-to-Market       | Sales model, channels, partnerships       | Job postings, press releases        |
| Technology         | Stack, patents, integrations              | Developer docs, patents, GitHub     |
| Team & Culture     | Leadership, hiring, Glassdoor             | LinkedIn, job boards, reviews       |
| Financial          | Revenue, funding, growth rate             | Crunchbase, SEC filings, press      |
| Customer           | Reviews, case studies, churn signals      | G2, Trustpilot, social media        |

### Analysis Depth Levels

| Level      | Time Investment | Output                    | When to Use                     |
|------------|-----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Quick scan | 2-4 hours       | 1-page summary            | New competitor appeared          |
| Standard   | 1-2 days        | Full competitive brief    | Quarterly review                 |
| Deep dive  | 1-2 weeks       | Comprehensive report      | Market entry, fundraise, M&A     |
| Ongoing    | 2 hours/week    | Monitoring updates        | Continuous intelligence          |

```
   | Brand loyalty        | H / M / L   | [Switching costs]              |
   | Regulatory barriers  | H / M / L   | [Licenses, certifications]     |
   | Technology barriers  | H / M / L   | [Patents, proprietary tech]    |
   | Network effects      | H / M / L   | [User base advantage]          |

   | Switching costs             | H / M / L  | [Lock-in effects]        |
   | Supplier differentiation    | H / M / L  | [Unique inputs]          |
   | Forward integration threat  | H / M / L  | [Supplier becoming rival]|

   Overall Assessment: [Summary]

   | Factor                      | Assessment | Impact                   |
   |-----------------------------|------------|--------------------------|
   | Buyer concentration         | H / M / L  | [Few large buyers]       |
   | Price sensitivity           | H / M / L  | [Budget constraints]     |
   | Switching costs             | H / M / L  | [Migration difficulty]   |
   | Information availability    | H / M / L  | [Comparison ease]        |
   | Backward integration threat | H / M / L  | [Build vs buy]           |

   Overall Assessment: [Summary]

4. THREAT OF SUBSTITUTES: [High / Medium / Low]

   | Substitute             | Price/Performance | Switching Cost | Risk Level |
   |------------------------|-------------------|----------------|------------|
   | [Substitute 1]         | Better / Same / Worse | H / M / L  | H / M / L  |

   | Factor                      | Assessment | Impact                   |

   | Exit barriers               | H / M / L  | [Sunk costs, contracts]  |
   | Strategic stakes            | H / M / L  | [Must-win markets]       |

   Overall Assessment: [Summary]

STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS:
[2-3 paragraphs on what these forces mean for strategy]
```

## Workflow

1. **Define scope**: Which competitors, which dimensions, what depth level
2. **Gather data**: Use the data sources listed in the analysis framework
3. **Apply frameworks**: Run Porter's Five Forces, SWOT, feature and pricing comparisons
4. **Synthesize findings**: Identify patterns, gaps, and opportunities
5. **Assess implications**: What does this mean for our strategy and roadmap
6. **Draft brief**: Compile findings using the competitive brief template
7. **Review with stakeholders**: Validate with sales, product, and leadership
8. **Distribute**: Share via appropriate channels based on sensitivity
9. **Set monitoring**: Establish ongoing tracking cadence

## Best Practices

- Separate facts from opinions in every analysis. Label assumptions clearly.
- Update competitive intelligence at least quarterly; stale analysis is dangerous.
- Talk to customers who evaluated competitors; win/loss interviews are the richest data source.
- Track competitors' job postings to predict strategic moves 3-6 months early.
- Focus on 3-5 primary competitors, not every player in the market.
- Build battle cards for sales from competitive briefs, keeping them to one page per competitor.
- Monitor competitor review sites (G2, Capterra) for emerging complaints that signal opportunity.
- Do not underestimate "do nothing" or "build in-house" as competitors.
- Re-run strategic frameworks annually at minimum, or after major market shifts.
