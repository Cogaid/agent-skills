# Onboarding Checklist - Reference Guide

## Onboarding Research and Benchmarks

### Key Statistics

| Metric | Finding | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Retention impact | Organizations with strong onboarding improve new hire retention by 82% | Brandon Hall Group |
| Productivity impact | Strong onboarding improves productivity by over 70% | Brandon Hall Group |
| Early attrition | 20% of turnover occurs within the first 45 days | SHRM |
| Engagement | Employees who had a positive onboarding experience are 18x more likely to feel committed | BambooHR |
| Cost of bad onboarding | Average cost of losing an employee in first year: 50-200% of annual salary | SHRM |
| Time to productivity | Average time to full productivity: 8-12 months without structured onboarding; 4-6 months with | Aberdeen Group |

### The Four C's of Onboarding (Bauer, 2010)

| Level | Name | Description | Example Activities |
|-------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Compliance | Legal and policy-related rules and regulations | Paperwork, policy acknowledgment, safety training |
| 2 | Clarification | Ensuring the employee understands the job and expectations | Role overview, goals document, performance criteria |
| 3 | Culture | Understanding organizational norms (formal and informal) | Values discussion, team rituals, buddy system, social events |
| 4 | Connection | Building vital interpersonal relationships | Stakeholder meetings, cross-functional introductions, mentorship |

Most organizations achieve Level 1-2 but neglect Level 3-4, which are the primary drivers of engagement and retention.

## Pre-Boarding Best Practices

### The "Offer Acceptance to Day 1" Gap

Research shows the period between offer acceptance and start date is a critical risk window:

- 28% of new hires report considering backing out before Day 1 (Glassdoor)
- The primary causes: silence from the company, anxiety about the decision, and competing offers arriving late
- Pre-boarding engagement reduces renege rates by up to 50%

### Pre-Boarding Communication Timeline

| Timing | Communication | Owner | Channel |
|--------|-------------|-------|---------|
| Day of acceptance | Congratulations email with next steps | Recruiter | Email |
| Within 3 days | Personal welcome message from hiring manager | Manager | Email or video |
| 1 week after acceptance | Welcome kit shipped (for remote) or swag package | HR/People Ops | Physical mail |
| 2 weeks before start | Logistics email (Day 1 details, parking, dress code) | HR/People Ops | Email |
| 1 week before start | Buddy introduction email | Buddy | Email |
| 2-3 days before start | Equipment delivery confirmation (remote) or desk assignment (office) | IT | Email |
| Day before start | "See you tomorrow" message with agenda | Manager | Email or Slack |

## 30-60-90 Day Framework Deep Dive

### Days 1-30: Learn Phase

The primary objective is contextual understanding, not output. New hires who are pressured to produce immediately have lower long-term performance.

**Cognitive Load Management:**
- New hires can absorb approximately 4 hours of new information per day before saturation
- Alternate between information intake (reading, meetings) and hands-on practice
- Provide written references for everything discussed verbally
- Do not schedule back-to-back meetings for the entire first week

**Relationship Building Targets:**
| Week | Target | Approach |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Meet all immediate team members | Manager introductions, team lunch |
| 2 | Meet key cross-functional partners | Scheduled 1:1 coffee chats |
| 3 | Meet skip-level manager and adjacent team leads | Scheduled introductions |
| 4 | Meet 5+ people outside your department | Self-directed with buddy guidance |

### Days 31-60: Contribute Phase

Shift from learning to doing. The new hire should be taking on real work with decreasing supervision.

**Independence Progression:**
| Activity | Week 1-2 | Week 3-4 | Week 5-6 | Week 7-8 |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Core tasks | Shadow / observe | Paired execution | Independent with review | Independent |
| Meetings | Attend and observe | Contribute when asked | Proactively contribute | Lead segments |
| Decision-making | Defer to others | Suggest options | Make small decisions | Make medium decisions |
| Problem-solving | Ask immediately | Research first, then ask | Solve and report | Solve independently |

### Days 61-90: Own Phase

The new hire should be operating as a full team member, identifying improvements, and setting their own goals.

**90-Day Review Framework:**

The 90-day review should answer three questions:
1. Is this person performing at the expected level for their role?
2. Is this person integrating well with the team and culture?
3. Is this person on a trajectory to succeed long-term?

If the answer to any of these is "no," early intervention is critical. Do not wait for a performance review cycle.

## Buddy System Research

### Why Buddies Matter

| Metric | With Buddy | Without Buddy | Source |
|--------|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Time to proficiency | 25% faster | Baseline | Microsoft internal study |
| New hire satisfaction | 36% higher | Baseline | HCI research |
| Retention at 1 year | 23% higher | Baseline | SHRM |
| Context questions asked | 2x more likely to ask | Baseline | Cornell University |

### Buddy vs. Mentor vs. Manager

| Role | Buddy | Mentor | Manager |
|------|-------|--------|---------|
| Duration | 90 days | 6-12 months | Ongoing |
| Focus | Day-to-day navigation | Career development | Performance and goals |
| Level | Peer | Senior (often cross-team) | Direct supervisor |
| Formality | Informal | Semi-formal | Formal |
| Frequency | Daily (Week 1), then tapering | Monthly | Weekly |
| Topics | "Where is the good coffee?" to "How does this process work?" | "How do I grow here?" to "What should I learn?" | "What should I deliver?" to "How am I doing?" |

### Buddy Selection and Training

**Selection Criteria (in priority order):**
1. Voluntarily opts in (never assign unwilling buddies)
2. Has 6-12 months tenure (remembers being new, knows enough to help)
3. Same or adjacent team (can answer contextual questions)
4. Peer level (creates psychological safety)
5. Strong communication skills (patient, approachable)
6. Different background from new hire (provides diverse perspective)

**Buddy Training Topics (30-minute session):**
- Role expectations and time commitment
- Conversation starters for each phase
- Boundaries (buddy is not the manager; do not evaluate performance)
- Escalation path (when to involve the manager or HR)
- How to navigate sensitive questions ("Is this a good place to work?")

## Remote Onboarding Adaptations

### Challenges Unique to Remote Onboarding

| Challenge | Impact | Mitigation |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Isolation | New hire feels disconnected from team | Daily video check-ins Week 1; virtual social events |
| Information access | Cannot overhear or ask quick questions | Comprehensive documentation; dedicated Slack channel |
| Technology setup | Cannot walk to IT desk | Ship equipment 5+ days early; IT support slot on Day 1 |
| Culture absorption | Misses informal cues and rituals | Buddy system; virtual office tours; culture documentation |
| Manager visibility | Hard to assess engagement and struggles | More frequent 1:1s; explicit "how are you feeling?" check-ins |
| Onboarding fatigue | Back-to-back Zoom calls all day | Cap video meetings at 4 hours/day; build in async time |

### Remote-Specific Checklist Additions

- [ ] Ship equipment 5+ days before start date with tracking
- [ ] Send a physical welcome kit (company swag, handwritten note)
- [ ] Schedule a "virtual office tour" -- walkthrough of key tools, channels, and docs
- [ ] Set up a dedicated Slack/Teams channel for the new hire's questions
- [ ] Schedule virtual coffee chats with 5+ team members in Week 1
- [ ] Pair with buddy for a 30-minute video call on Day 1
- [ ] Record key orientation sessions for async review
- [ ] Schedule a "questions I'm afraid to ask" session with buddy in Week 2

## Onboarding Metrics and Measurement

### Leading Indicators (Measure During Onboarding)

| Metric | Target | How to Measure | Alert Threshold |
|--------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Task completion rate | 90%+ by Day 30 | Onboarding checklist tracking | Below 70% by Day 30 |
| Buddy check-in frequency | Per schedule | Buddy log / survey | Missed 2+ consecutive |
| Manager 1:1 frequency | Weekly | Calendar audit | Missed 2+ in first 30 days |
| Tool access completion | 100% by Day 3 | IT tracking | Any tool missing after Day 5 |
| Week 1 survey score | 4+/5 | Survey | Below 3/5 on any question |

### Lagging Indicators (Measure Post-Onboarding)

| Metric | Target | How to Measure | Benchmark |
|--------|--------|---------------|-----------|
| 90-day retention rate | 95%+ | HRIS | Industry average: 85-90% |
| Time to productivity | <6 months | Manager assessment | Without onboarding: 8-12 months |
| 90-day survey NPS | 50+ | Survey | Average company NPS: 30-40 |
| 6-month engagement score | Above company average | Engagement survey | Compare to tenured employees |
| 1-year retention rate | 85%+ | HRIS | Industry average: 70-80% |

### Onboarding Program ROI Calculation

```
ROI = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs x 100

Benefits:
- Reduced early attrition savings = (attrition reduction) x (cost per departure)
- Faster productivity = (weeks saved) x (weekly salary) x (productivity gap %)
- Higher engagement = (engagement score improvement) x (productivity correlation)

Costs:
- Program design and materials
- Buddy/mentor time (hours x hourly rate)
- Manager time (additional 1:1s beyond normal)
- Technology and tools
- Training content development

Typical ROI: 200-500% for well-designed programs
```

## Role-Specific Onboarding Extensions

### Engineering Onboarding Extras

**Development Environment Setup Checklist:**
- [ ] Clone and build the main repository
- [ ] Run the test suite successfully
- [ ] Deploy to staging environment
- [ ] Set up local debugging tools
- [ ] Configure IDE with team-standard settings
- [ ] Access monitoring and logging dashboards
- [ ] Review and understand the on-call runbook

**First Contribution Path:**
| Week | Activity | Goal |
|------|----------|------|
| 1 | Read code, attend code reviews | Understand codebase and standards |
| 2 | Fix a labeled "good first issue" bug | First PR merged |
| 3 | Take on a small feature or improvement | Independent contribution |
| 4 | Participate in a design review | Understand decision-making process |

### Sales Onboarding Extras

**Product Knowledge Ramp:**
| Week | Topic | Assessment |
|------|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Company story, market, and competitors | Quiz |
| 2 | Product features and use cases | Demo to manager |
| 3 | Pricing, packaging, and objection handling | Role play |
| 4 | CRM workflow and pipeline management | Practical exercise |

**Call Shadow Schedule:**
- Week 1-2: Shadow 2 calls per day across different reps and deal stages
- Week 3: Co-pilot calls with manager providing real-time coaching
- Week 4: Solo calls with manager listening and providing post-call feedback

### Manager Onboarding Extras

**Listening Tour Template:**
Meet with 10-15 stakeholders in the first 30 days. Ask each:
1. What is working well that I should not change?
2. What is the biggest challenge you face?
3. What do you need from my team that you are not getting?
4. What should I know that nobody will tell me?
5. How can I best support you?
