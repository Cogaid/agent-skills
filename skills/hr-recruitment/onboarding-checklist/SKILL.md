# Onboarding Checklist

Build structured onboarding programs that take new hires from offer acceptance to full productivity with clear milestones, accountability, and feedback loops.

## Purpose

Effective onboarding reduces time-to-productivity by 50% and improves new hire retention by 82% (Brandon Hall Group). Poor onboarding is the number one driver of early attrition, with 20% of turnover occurring in the first 45 days. This skill provides a complete onboarding framework from pre-boarding through the first 90 days, adaptable to any role, level, or organization size.

## Quick Reference

### 30-60-90 Day Framework

| Phase | Days | Theme | Goal |
|-------|------|-------|------|
| Pre-boarding | Offer to Day 1 | Preparation and welcome | New hire feels expected and excited |
| Week 1 | Days 1-5 | Orientation and immersion | Understand the company, team, and role basics |
| Days 1-30 | Learning | Absorb context, build relationships, deliver quick wins | Navigate independently within their domain |
| Days 31-60 | Contributing | Take on meaningful work, deepen expertise | Deliver measurable contributions |
| Days 61-90 | Owning | Operate independently, identify improvements | Function as a full team member with clear trajectory |

### Onboarding Accountability Matrix

| Stakeholder | Responsibilities |
|-------------|-----------------|
| HR / People Ops | Compliance paperwork, benefits enrollment, systems access, orientation logistics |
| Hiring Manager | Role expectations, goals, 1:1 cadence, feedback, integration into team |
| Onboarding Buddy | Day-to-day questions, cultural navigation, informal mentorship |
| IT / Operations | Equipment, accounts, tool access, security setup |
| New Hire | Active learning, asking questions, completing onboarding tasks, seeking feedback |
| Skip-Level Manager | Welcome meeting, organizational context, career path discussion |

## Pre-Boarding Checklist (Offer Acceptance to Day 1)

### HR / People Ops Tasks

- [ ] Send welcome email with Day 1 logistics (time, location/link, dress code, parking)
- [ ] Mail or ship welcome kit (swag, handbook, equipment if remote)
- [ ] Initiate background check and track completion
- [ ] Prepare and send employment paperwork (I-9, W-4, direct deposit, NDA)
- [ ] Set up payroll and benefits enrollment
- [ ] Create employee record in HRIS
- [ ] Schedule orientation sessions (company overview, benefits review, compliance training)
- [ ] Notify reception / security for building access

### Hiring Manager Tasks

- [ ] Send personal welcome message (email or video)
- [ ] Assign an onboarding buddy
- [ ] Draft 30-60-90 day goals document
- [ ] Schedule recurring 1:1 meetings (weekly for first 90 days)
- [ ] Prepare team introduction plan
- [ ] Identify first project or quick-win assignment
- [ ] Brief the team on the new hire's background and start date
- [ ] Schedule key stakeholder intro meetings for Week 1-2

### IT / Operations Tasks

- [ ] Order and configure laptop/equipment
- [ ] Create email and corporate accounts
- [ ] Set up access to required tools (see Tools Access Checklist below)
- [ ] Prepare VPN and security credentials
- [ ] Ship equipment to remote employees (arrive 2+ days before start)
- [ ] Create accounts in project management and communication tools

## First Week Milestones

- [ ] Complete all compliance and HR paperwork
- [ ] Access all required tools and systems successfully
- [ ] Meet all immediate team members
- [ ] Meet onboarding buddy and establish check-in rhythm
- [ ] Review team goals, OKRs, and current projects
- [ ] Attend first team standup / meeting
- [ ] Complete mandatory training modules (security, harassment prevention, etc.)
- [ ] Understand the team communication norms (Slack channels, email, meetings)
- [ ] Read key documentation (product docs, architecture guides, runbooks)
- [ ] Have first 1:1 with hiring manager to review expectations

## First Month Milestones (Days 1-30)

- [ ] Complete all onboarding training modules
- [ ] Ship first deliverable or quick win
- [ ] Attend cross-functional meetings and understand key stakeholders
- [ ] Understand the team's workflow and development process
- [ ] Build relationships with 5-10 colleagues outside the immediate team
- [ ] Complete first self-assessment on onboarding experience
- [ ] Receive first informal feedback from manager
- [ ] Identify one area of confusion or friction and raise it
- [ ] Understand how performance is measured and reviewed

## Second Month Milestones (Days 31-60)

- [ ] Take ownership of a meaningful project or workstream
- [ ] Contribute independently to team deliverables
- [ ] Present work or insights to the team
- [ ] Deepen domain knowledge through documentation and mentorship
- [ ] Build cross-functional relationships
- [ ] Receive structured feedback from manager (midpoint check-in)
- [ ] Begin contributing to process improvement
- [ ] Reduce dependency on buddy for day-to-day questions
- [ ] Identify personal learning goals and development areas

## Third Month Milestones (Days 61-90)

- [ ] Operate independently on core responsibilities
- [ ] Deliver a measurable business outcome
- [ ] Propose and implement at least one improvement
- [ ] Complete 90-day review with manager
- [ ] Transition from onboarding buddy to standard mentorship (if applicable)
- [ ] Set goals for the next quarter
- [ ] Provide feedback on the onboarding process (survey)
- [ ] Achieve proficiency in key tools and systems
- [ ] Demonstrate alignment with team values and working norms

## Buddy System Guidelines

### Who Should Be a Buddy

| Criteria | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Same or adjacent team | Can answer day-to-day questions with context |
| 6+ months tenure | Knows enough to be helpful but remembers being new |
| Peer level (not manager) | Creates psychological safety for "dumb" questions |
| Volunteer, not conscripted | Enthusiasm matters |
| Different background from new hire | Provides diverse perspective on the organization |

### Buddy Responsibilities

| Timeframe | Actions |
|-----------|---------|
| Pre-start | Send a personal intro message, share tips for Day 1 |
| Week 1 | Daily 15-minute check-ins, office/virtual tour, introduce to key people |
| Weeks 2-4 | Twice-weekly check-ins, answer questions, provide cultural context |
| Weeks 5-8 | Weekly check-ins, help navigate cross-functional relationships |
| Weeks 9-12 | As-needed check-ins, transition to peer relationship |

### Buddy Conversation Starters

```
Week 1: "What questions do you have that you feel silly asking?"
Week 2: "Who else should you meet? I can make introductions."
Week 3: "What has surprised you most about working here so far?"
Week 4: "Is there anything about the team culture that is unclear?"
Week 6: "What do you need to be more effective in your role?"
Week 8: "What would you change about the onboarding experience?"
```

## Role-Specific Onboarding Variations

### Engineering

- [ ] Set up local development environment
- [ ] Complete first code review (reading, not writing)
- [ ] Submit first pull request (bug fix or small improvement)
- [ ] Review architecture documentation and system diagrams
- [ ] Shadow on-call rotation for one cycle
- [ ] Pair program with 2-3 different team members
- [ ] Complete security training for production access

### Sales

- [ ] Complete product knowledge certification
- [ ] Shadow 5+ customer calls in Week 1-2
- [ ] Role-play sales scenarios with manager
- [ ] Learn CRM workflows and pipeline management
- [ ] Review top 10 customer accounts and case studies
- [ ] Make first solo customer call by Day 30
- [ ] Build first pipeline by Day 60

### Management / Leadership

- [ ] Meet every direct report 1:1 in Week 1
- [ ] Review team performance data and recent reviews
- [ ] Understand current team goals and blockers
- [ ] Meet peer managers and key cross-functional partners
- [ ] Establish leadership cadence (team meetings, 1:1s, skip-levels)
- [ ] Complete listening tour with 10+ stakeholders by Day 30
- [ ] Present 90-day observations and plan to skip-level

## Templates

### Onboarding Feedback Survey (End of Week 1)

```
1. How welcome did you feel on your first day? (1-5)
2. Was your workspace/equipment ready when you arrived? (Yes/No)
3. Do you have access to all the tools you need? (Yes/No, list gaps)
4. How clear are your role expectations? (1-5)
5. How helpful has your onboarding buddy been? (1-5)
6. What has been the best part of your first week?
7. What could be improved about the onboarding experience?
8. Is there anything you need that you have not received?
```

### Onboarding Feedback Survey (End of 90 Days)

```
1. How prepared do you feel to do your job effectively? (1-5)
2. How well do you understand the company's mission and values? (1-5)
3. How supported do you feel by your manager? (1-5)
4. How connected do you feel to your team? (1-5)
5. How clear is your path for growth and development? (1-5)
6. Would you recommend this company to a friend? (1-10 NPS)
7. What was the most valuable part of your onboarding?
8. What was missing from your onboarding experience?
9. How long did it take you to feel productive? (weeks)
10. Any additional feedback or suggestions?
```

## Best Practices

1. **Start before Day 1.** Pre-boarding sets the tone. A silent gap between offer acceptance and start date causes buyer's remorse.
2. **Front-load relationships, not paperwork.** The first day should feel human, not bureaucratic. Move paperwork to pre-boarding.
3. **Assign a buddy, not just a manager.** New hires need a safe peer for "obvious" questions they do not want to ask their boss.
4. **Set clear 30-60-90 goals before the start date.** Ambiguity in the first weeks leads to anxiety and disengagement.
5. **Check in frequently.** Manager 1:1s should be weekly for the first 90 days, minimum.
6. **Celebrate small wins.** Acknowledge the first PR, first customer call, or first presentation publicly.
7. **Collect feedback and iterate.** Use onboarding surveys to improve the process for the next hire.
8. **Do not overload Week 1.** Information overload reduces retention. Spread learning over 30 days.
9. **Make it two-way.** Ask what the new hire brings, not just what they need to learn. Fresh eyes are valuable.
10. **Customize by role.** A one-size-fits-all onboarding ignores the different needs of engineers, salespeople, and managers.
