# Job Description Writer

Generate structured, inclusive, and high-converting job descriptions tailored to role level, industry, and hiring goals.

## Purpose

A well-crafted job description is the single highest-leverage artifact in the hiring funnel. It determines who applies, sets expectations for the role, and reflects the employer brand. This skill produces JDs that attract diverse, qualified candidates while remaining legally compliant and optimized for job board search algorithms.

## Quick Reference

### JD Structure Framework

| Section | Purpose | Ideal Length |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Job Title | Searchable, accurate title | 3-6 words |
| About Us | Employer brand hook | 3-5 sentences |
| Role Summary | What the person will do and why it matters | 2-4 sentences |
| Responsibilities | Day-to-day duties | 5-8 bullet points |
| Requirements | Must-have qualifications | 4-6 bullet points |
| Nice-to-Haves | Preferred but not required | 3-5 bullet points |
| Compensation & Benefits | Total rewards | 4-8 bullet points |
| How to Apply | Clear next step | 1-3 sentences |
| EEO Statement | Legal compliance | 1-2 sentences |

### Inclusive Language Guidelines

| Avoid | Use Instead | Reason |
|-------|------------|--------|
| "rockstar," "ninja," "guru" | "experienced," "skilled," "specialist" | Gendered/exclusionary connotation |
| "young and energetic" | "motivated and enthusiastic" | Age discrimination |
| "must have 10+ years" | "deep experience with..." | Screens out non-traditional paths |
| "culture fit" | "culture add" or "values alignment" | Bias toward homogeneity |
| "he/she" | "you" or "they" | Non-binary inclusive |
| "native English speaker" | "fluent in English" | National origin discrimination |
| "able-bodied" | Remove or describe actual requirement | Disability discrimination |
| "manpower" | "workforce," "team" | Gendered language |
| "digital native" | "comfortable with technology" | Age discrimination |
| "aggressive" | "ambitious," "driven" | Masculine-coded language |

### Masculine vs. Feminine Coded Words

Research shows that masculine-coded language reduces female applicants by up to 30%.

| Masculine-Coded (reduce) | Feminine-Coded (balance) | Neutral (prefer) |
|--------------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Competitive | Collaborative | Results-oriented |
| Dominant | Supportive | Effective |
| Assertive | Empathetic | Skilled |
| Independent | Community | Autonomous |
| Ambitious | Loyal | Growth-minded |

## Workflow

### Step 1: Intake

- [ ] Confirm role title and department
- [ ] Identify hiring manager and interview panel
- [ ] Clarify level (junior / mid / senior / lead / executive)
- [ ] Determine employment type (full-time, part-time, contract)
- [ ] Confirm location and remote policy
- [ ] Establish compensation range and budget
- [ ] Identify 3-5 key outcomes for the first year
- [ ] List must-have vs. nice-to-have qualifications
- [ ] Note any compliance requirements (clearance, certifications)

### Step 2: Draft

- [ ] Write title using standard, searchable terminology
- [ ] Draft About Us section aligned with employer brand
- [ ] Write role summary connecting to company mission
- [ ] List responsibilities in priority order
- [ ] Separate hard requirements from nice-to-haves
- [ ] Include compensation range (improves apply rate by 30%)
- [ ] Add benefits and perks
- [ ] Include EEO statement

### Step 3: Review

- [ ] Run inclusive language check (see guidelines above)
- [ ] Verify requirements list has no more than 6-8 items
- [ ] Confirm reading level is grade 8-10 (Flesch-Kincaid)
- [ ] Have diverse team members review for bias
- [ ] Verify legal compliance with local regulations
- [ ] Check SEO keywords match job board search terms

### Step 4: Publish

- [ ] Post to ATS and company careers page
- [ ] Distribute to job boards with appropriate tags
- [ ] Share on social channels with tailored messaging
- [ ] Set review cadence (refresh every 30 days if unfilled)

## Templates

### Template: About Us Section

```
[Company Name] is a [industry] company that [core mission in one sentence].
Founded in [year], we serve [customer description] by [value proposition].
Our team of [size] is driven by [core values - pick 2-3]. We believe that
[diversity/inclusion statement]. We are [growth stage / recent milestone].
```

### Template: Junior/Entry-Level Role

```

**Location:** [City, State | Remote | Hybrid]
**Employment Type:** Full-time
**Compensation:** $[X]-$[Y] annually

### About [Company Name]
[About Us section]

### The Opportunity
We are looking for a [Title] to join our [Department] team. In this role,
you will [2-3 sentence summary of impact]. This is an excellent opportunity
for someone early in their career who is eager to learn and grow.

### What You Will Do
- [Responsibility with learning component]
- [Responsibility with mentorship angle]
- [Responsibility tied to team deliverable]
- [Responsibility showing growth path]
- [Responsibility with cross-functional exposure]

### What We Are Looking For
- [Degree or equivalent practical experience]
- [1-2 foundational technical skills]
- [Communication or collaboration skill]
- [Curiosity/learning orientation]

### Nice to Have
- [Relevant internship or project experience]
- [Familiarity with specific tool or methodology]
- [Industry knowledge]

### What We Offer
- Competitive salary and benefits package
- [Mentorship / training program]
- [Specific perk 1]
- [Specific perk 2]
- [Growth/promotion path]

[Company Name] is an equal opportunity employer. We celebrate diversity and
are committed to creating an inclusive environment for all employees.
```

## SEO Optimization for Job Boards

### Title Optimization

| Platform | Best Practice | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| LinkedIn | Use standard titles, skip internal jargon | "Senior Software Engineer" not "Code Wizard III" |
| Indeed | Include level + core function | "Marketing Manager - Digital Advertising" |
| Glassdoor | Add location or remote tag | "Product Designer (Remote)" |
| Google Jobs | Use schema-friendly titles | Match O*NET standard occupational titles |

### Keyword Strategy

1. Research top search terms on target job boards for the role
2. Include primary keyword in title, first paragraph, and 2-3 bullet points
3. Use natural variations (e.g., "software engineer," "developer," "SWE")
4. Include technology stack and tools by name
5. Add industry-specific terms candidates would search for

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Impact | Fix |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Listing 15+ requirements | Qualified candidates self-select out | Cap at 6-8 must-haves |
| No salary range | 30-50% fewer applicants | Always include compensation range |
| Internal jargon in title | Low search visibility | Use industry-standard titles |
| Wall of text | Candidates stop reading | Use bullets, headers, whitespace |
| Copy-paste from old JD | Outdated requirements, stale language | Rewrite for each opening |
| Requiring degree for all roles | Excludes qualified non-traditional candidates | Add "or equivalent experience" |
| Vague responsibilities | Mismatched expectations | Tie to specific outcomes |

## Best Practices

1. **Lead with impact, not tasks.** Candidates want to know why the role matters, not just what they will do.
2. **Show the career path.** Mention growth opportunities and what success looks like at 6-12 months.
3. **Be honest about challenges.** Authenticity builds trust and reduces early attrition.
4. **Keep requirements to true must-haves.** Research shows women apply when meeting 100% of requirements; men apply at 60%.
5. **Update regularly.** A stale JD signals a stale role. Refresh every 30 days if the position remains open.
6. **Include compensation.** Transparency attracts more and better-matched applicants.
7. **Test and iterate.** Track apply rates per JD version and improve over time.
