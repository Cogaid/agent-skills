### Top Reasons Employees Leave (Meta-Analysis)

Research from Gallup, SHRM, and Work Institute consistently identifies these top drivers:

| Rank | Reason | % Citing as Primary | Addressable? |
|------|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Career development / growth opportunities | 22% | Yes |
| 2 | Manager relationship / quality | 18% | Yes |
| 3 | Compensation / benefits | 15% | Partially |
| 4 | Work-life balance / burnout | 13% | Yes |
| 5 | Company culture / values misalignment | 10% | Partially |
| 6 | Job fit / role mismatch | 8% | Yes (through better hiring) |
| 7 | Recognition / feeling valued | 7% | Yes |
| 8 | Personal / relocation | 5% | No |
| 9 | External opportunity (pulled away) | 2% | Partially |

Key insight: Approximately 75% of voluntary attrition is driven by factors the organization can influence.

### The Departure Timeline

Research shows that employees typically go through a predictable disengagement sequence:

| Stage | Timeline (Before Departure) | Observable Signals |
|-------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Trigger Event | 12-18 months | Specific incident sparks disengagement (passed over for promotion, manager conflict, reorg) |
| Quiet Disengagement | 6-12 months | Reduced discretionary effort, fewer ideas, less participation in optional activities |
| Active Job Search | 3-6 months | Updated LinkedIn, taking PTO for interviews, reduced long-term planning |
| Mental Checkout | 1-3 months | Minimum viable work, knowledge hoarding, emotional detachment |
| Resignation | 0 | Formal notice |

Implication: By the time an employee resigns, the root cause likely occurred 12-18 months earlier. Exit interviews should probe for the original trigger, not just the proximate cause.

## Exit Interview Methodology

### Interview vs. Survey vs. Both

| Method | Response Rate | Data Quality | Scalability | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| In-person interview | 85-95% | Highest (nuanced, follow-up) | Low | Senior roles, high-value employees |
| Video interview | 80-90% | High | Medium | Remote employees, mid-level |
| Phone interview | 70-85% | Medium-High | Medium | Broad coverage |
| Online survey | 40-60% | Medium (no follow-up) | High | Large organizations, all levels |
| Combined (survey + interview) | 60-80% | Highest | Medium | Recommended approach |

**Recommended approach:** Send an online survey 2 weeks before departure, then conduct a 45-minute interview in the final week. The survey provides structured data; the interview provides depth and context.

### Interviewer Selection

| Interviewer | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|------------|------|------|----------|
| HR Business Partner | Trusted, experienced, can spot patterns | May be perceived as "company side" | Standard exits |
| Third-party consultant | Maximum candor, perceived neutrality | Cost, less organizational context | Senior exits, sensitive situations |
| Skip-level manager | Strategic context, shows organizational care | May feel like a performance discussion | Leadership departures |
| Peer in another department | Relatable, informal | Less trained in interviewing | Supplementary perspective |
| Direct manager | **Avoid** | Employees will not be candid | Never for exit interviews |

### Interview Facilitation Techniques

**Building Rapport:**
- Start with: "Everything you share today is confidential and will only be reported in aggregate. Your name will not be attached to specific feedback."
- Acknowledge: "We value your time here and want to learn from your experience."
- Frame: "Your candid feedback helps us improve for the people who stay."

**Probing for Root Cause:**
Use the "5 Whys" technique to get past surface answers:

1. "Why are you leaving?" -- "I got a better offer."
2. "Why were you looking?" -- "I felt stuck in my career."
3. "Why did you feel stuck?" -- "My manager never discussed growth with me."
4. "Why do you think those conversations did not happen?" -- "She was always focused on deliverables, not development."
5. "Why do you think the organization allowed that?" -- "There is no accountability for manager quality."

The fifth "why" often reveals the systemic issue.

## Data Analysis Framework

### Quantitative Analysis

**Category Frequency Analysis:**
- Calculate the percentage of exits citing each category (MGT, CMP, CDV, etc.)
- A category cited in 30%+ of exits is a systemic issue
- A category trending upward over 3+ quarters is an emerging risk

**Segmentation Dimensions:**
| Dimension | Why It Matters | What to Look For |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Department / Team | Identifies hotspot teams | Teams with 2x+ average attrition |
| Manager | Surfaces manager quality issues | Managers with 3+ direct reports departing in 12 months |
| Tenure band | Reveals lifecycle problems | Clusters at specific tenure points |
| Level | Identifies systemic issues by seniority | High attrition at a specific level |
| Performance rating | Measures regrettable attrition | High performers leaving at higher rates |
| Demographics | Equity analysis | Disproportionate attrition in any group |
| Hire source | Evaluates recruiting quality | Certain hire sources with higher attrition |

### Qualitative Analysis

**Thematic Coding Process:**
1. Read all open-ended responses for the period
2. Identify recurring themes using the category framework
3. Code each response (a single response may have multiple codes)
4. Count theme frequency and identify co-occurrences
5. Extract representative quotes (anonymized) for reporting

**Sentiment Analysis:**
For each response category, assess the tone:
| Sentiment | Description | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| Positive | Positive experience in this area | "My manager was great -- one of the best I have had" |
| Neutral | Neither positive nor negative | "The benefits were standard" |
| Negative | Negative experience, moderate intensity | "I wish there had been more growth opportunities" |
| Strongly Negative | Significant dissatisfaction | "The culture was toxic and leadership did not care" |

### Push vs. Pull Analysis

| Factor Type | Definition | Example | Intervention Type |
|------------|-----------|---------|------------------|
| Push (Internal) | Negative internal factors driving the employee away | Bad manager, low pay, no growth | Fix the internal problem |
| Pull (External) | Positive external factors attracting the employee | Dream company, higher salary, exciting product | Improve competitiveness |
| Both | Internal dissatisfaction + external attraction | Underpaid AND received a 30% raise elsewhere | Address both dimensions |

Most departures involve both push and pull factors. However, research shows that push factors must exist before pull factors become decisive. An employee who is happy will not take a recruiter's call.

## Cost of Attrition Model

### Detailed Cost Components

| Cost Category | Junior (< $80K) | Mid ($80K-$150K) | Senior ($150K-$250K) | Executive ($250K+) |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Recruiting (sourcing, agency, tools) | $5K-$10K | $10K-$25K | $25K-$50K | $50K-$150K |
| Interviewing (team time) | $2K-$5K | $5K-$10K | $10K-$20K | $20K-$40K |
| Onboarding + training | $3K-$8K | $8K-$15K | $15K-$25K | $25K-$50K |
| Lost productivity (vacant role) | $5K-$15K/mo | $15K-$30K/mo | $30K-$50K/mo | $50K-$100K/mo |
| Ramp-up (new hire at reduced output) | $5K-$10K | $10K-$25K | $25K-$50K | $50K-$100K |
| Knowledge loss | $2K-$10K | $10K-$30K | $30K-$100K | $100K-$500K |
| Team impact (overtime, morale) | $2K-$5K | $5K-$15K | $15K-$30K | $30K-$50K |
| **Total Estimated** | **$25K-$65K** | **$65K-$160K** | **$160K-$350K** | **$350K-$1M+** |

Rule of thumb by level:
- Junior: 50-75% of annual salary
- Mid: 75-125% of annual salary
- Senior: 100-150% of annual salary
- Executive: 150-300% of annual salary

### ROI of Retention Interventions

| Intervention | Annual Cost | Expected Impact | Payback Period |
|-------------|------------|----------------|---------------|
| Manager training program | $500-$2K per manager | 15-25% reduction in manager-driven attrition | 3-6 months |
| Market compensation adjustment | 5-15% of affected payroll | 20-40% reduction in comp-driven attrition | 6-12 months |
| Career ladder / development program | $50K-$200K to build | 15-30% reduction in growth-driven attrition | 6-18 months |
| Stay interviews (proactive) | 2-3 hours per employee/year | 10-20% reduction in overall attrition | 3-6 months |

| Recognition program | $10K-$50K/year | 10-15% reduction in recognition-driven attrition | 3-6 months |

## Retention Strategy Playbook

### Stay Interviews (Proactive Retention)

Stay interviews are the proactive counterpart to exit interviews. Conduct them quarterly with high-value employees.

**Core Stay Interview Questions:**
1. What do you look forward to when you come to work each day?
2. What are you learning here? What do you want to learn?
3. Why do you stay here?
4. When was the last time you thought about leaving? What prompted it?
5. What can I do more of or less of as your manager?
6. What would tempt you to leave?
7. What is not being fully utilized about your talent?

### Retention Risk Scoring

For each employee, assess:

| Factor | Low Risk (1) | Medium Risk (2) | High Risk (3) |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Market demand for their skills | Low demand | Moderate demand | Hot market |
| Tenure | <1 year or >5 years | 1-2 years | 2-4 years (peak risk) |
| Recent life changes | None | Minor (new home, etc.) | Major (spouse relocated, etc.) |
| Manager relationship | Strong | Adequate | Strained |
| Recent recognition | Frequent | Occasional | Rare or none |
| Compensation vs. market | Above market | At market | Below market |
| Career progression | Recent promotion or clear path | Stable | Stagnant or passed over |
| Engagement signals | High participation | Normal | Declining or withdrawn |

**Score interpretation:**
- 8-12: Low risk. Monitor normally.
- 13-18: Medium risk. Proactive stay interview recommended.
- 19-24: High risk. Immediate retention intervention required.

## Reporting and Visualization

### Executive Summary Best Practices

1. **Lead with the number, then the narrative.** "Attrition is 18%, up from 14%. Here is why."
2. **Show trends, not snapshots.** Three quarters minimum for credibility.
3. **Segment to reveal the story.** Company-wide numbers hide team-level crises.
4. **Connect to cost.** Every insight should have a dollar sign attached.
5. **Propose solutions with ROI.** Do not present problems without actionable recommendations.
6. **Limit to 3-5 key findings.** Executives will not absorb more than that.

### Recommended Visualizations

| Visualization | What It Shows | When to Use |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Trend line chart | Attrition rate over time | Every report |
| Stacked bar chart | Category frequency by quarter | Every report |
| Heat map | Attrition by team/department | When team-level variation exists |
| Pareto chart | Top reasons ranked by frequency | Prioritization discussions |
| Scatter plot | Tenure vs. attrition rate | Lifecycle analysis |
| Sankey diagram | Flow from reasons to departments | Complex multi-factor analysis |
| Waterfall chart | Cost of attrition breakdown | Budget discussions |

### Confidentiality and Ethics

| Principle | Practice |
|-----------|---------|
| Anonymity | Never attribute feedback to individuals in reports |
| Minimum threshold | Do not report team-level data when fewer than 5 departures (risk of identification) |
| Secure storage | Exit interview data stored in encrypted, access-controlled systems |
| Retention policy | Retain exit data for 3-5 years for trend analysis; purge per data governance policy |
| Transparency | Inform departing employees how their data will be used |
| Action obligation | If urgent issues are disclosed (harassment, safety), act immediately regardless of confidentiality |
