# Offer Letter Writer - Reference Guide

## Compensation Structure Deep Dive

### Base Salary Determination

Base salary is the fixed component of compensation. Key factors in setting base salary:

| Factor | Impact | How to Evaluate |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Market data | Primary driver | Surveys (Radford, Mercer), crowdsourced (levels.fyi, Glassdoor) |
| Internal equity | Must avoid disparities | Compare to existing employees at same level/role |
| Geographic adjustment | Significant variation | Use cost-of-labor (not cost-of-living) adjustments |
| Budget constraints | Hard ceiling | Verify headcount and comp budget approval |
| Candidate leverage | Can push above midpoint | Competing offers, unique skills, current compensation |
| Role criticality | Justifies premium | Revenue impact, hard-to-fill, specialized expertise |

### Geographic Pay Differentials

Common approach: Set a "headquarters rate" and apply geographic multipliers.

| Zone | Example Markets | Typical Multiplier |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Zone 1 (Highest) | San Francisco, New York City | 1.00x (baseline) |
| Zone 2 | Seattle, Boston, Los Angeles, DC | 0.90-0.95x |
| Zone 3 | Austin, Denver, Chicago, Atlanta | 0.80-0.90x |
| Zone 4 | Midwest cities, smaller metros | 0.75-0.85x |
| Zone 5 | Rural US, low cost-of-living areas | 0.70-0.80x |
| International | Varies widely | Use local market data |

Important: "Cost of labor" (what employers pay for similar talent in a market) is preferred over "cost of living" (what it costs to live somewhere). These can diverge significantly.

### Equity Compensation Explained

#### Restricted Stock Units (RSUs)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| What they are | Company shares granted on a vesting schedule |
| Taxation | Taxed as ordinary income on each vesting date based on fair market value |
| Risk | Value fluctuates with stock price; worth zero if company fails |
| Typical vesting | 4 years with 1-year cliff, then monthly or quarterly |
| Public vs. private | Public companies: liquid on vesting. Private companies: not liquid until IPO or acquisition |

#### Stock Options (ISOs and NSOs)

| Feature | ISO (Incentive Stock Options) | NSO (Non-Qualified Stock Options) |
|---------|------|------|
| Tax at grant | None | None |
| Tax at exercise | No ordinary income tax (AMT may apply) | Ordinary income tax on spread |
| Tax at sale | Long-term capital gains if held 2yr from grant + 1yr from exercise | Capital gains on any appreciation after exercise |
| Who receives | Employees only | Employees, contractors, advisors |
| Annual limit | $100K exercisable per year (by grant date FMV) | No limit |
| Post-termination exercise | Typically 90 days | Varies, can be extended |

#### Equity Communication Tips

- Always present equity value as a range, not a point estimate
- For private companies, explain that equity is illiquid and speculative
- Provide 409A valuation date and methodology context
- Include vesting schedule visualization in offer materials
- Explain dilution risk and what happens in various exit scenarios

### Signing Bonus Strategy

| Scenario | Recommended Signing Bonus | Rationale |
|----------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Candidate leaving unvested equity | Match forfeited equity value | Makes candidate whole |
| Below-band base salary (budget constraint) | Bridge the gap in year 1 | Candidate earns closer to market total comp |
| Highly competitive market | 10-20% of base | Differentiation and urgency |
| Relocation required | Cost of move + adjustment | Removes financial barrier |
| Counter to competing offer | Match or exceed competitor's signing bonus | Table stakes in negotiation |

**Clawback provisions:** Standard practice is to require repayment (pro-rated) if the employee voluntarily leaves within 12 months. Some organizations use 24-month clawbacks for larger bonuses. Always disclose clawback terms clearly.

## Negotiation Psychology

### Candidate Motivations Framework

Research on candidate decision-making identifies five primary motivators:

| Motivator | Weight (Avg) | What to Offer | How to Identify |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| Compensation | 30% | Competitive total comp | Market data, stated expectations |
| Growth / Learning | 25% | Career path, mentorship, scope | Interview responses, career trajectory |
| Mission / Impact | 20% | Clear connection to meaningful work | What excites them about the role |
| Work-Life Balance | 15% | Flexibility, reasonable workload | Questions about schedule, remote policy |
| Team / Culture | 10% | Team quality, management style | Questions about team, reference requests |

### Negotiation Best Practices

**The "Expandable Pie" Approach:**
Instead of treating negotiation as a zero-sum game on base salary, expand the pie by exploring all compensation levers:

1. Base salary (most expensive for the company, most taxed for the candidate)
2. Signing bonus (one-time cost, flexible)
3. Equity (aligns incentives, deferred cost)
4. Start date (zero cost, high value to candidates with transitions)
5. Title (zero cost, high perceived value)
6. Remote flexibility (potentially cost-saving for the company)
7. Learning budget (low cost, high development value)
8. Vacation days (moderate cost, high lifestyle value)

**Negotiation Do's and Don'ts:**

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Listen before responding | React emotionally to counteroffers |
| Ask "What is most important to you?" | Assume it is all about base salary |
| Provide rationale for your offer | Say "take it or leave it" |
| Respond within 24-48 hours | Let negotiations drag beyond a week |
| Put revised terms in writing | Make verbal promises you cannot keep |
| Know your walkaway number before starting | Exceed your approved budget without authorization |
| Celebrate the close | Treat acceptance as a transaction |

## Legal Compliance Deep Dive

### At-Will Employment

At-will employment means either the employer or employee can end the relationship at any time, for any lawful reason, with or without notice. Key considerations:

- **Montana exception:** Montana requires "good cause" for termination after a probationary period
- **Implied contract risk:** Language like "permanent position" or "job security" can create an implied contract. Avoid these terms.
- **Public policy exceptions:** Even at-will employees cannot be fired for illegal reasons (retaliation, discrimination, exercising legal rights)

**Recommended at-will language:**
```
Your employment with [Company] is at-will, meaning that either you or
[Company] may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any
reason, with or without cause or notice. Nothing in this letter or any
other document creates a contract of employment for any specific period.
```

### Non-Compete Agreement Landscape (US)

| Status | Jurisdictions |
|--------|--------------|
| Banned entirely | California, Minnesota, North Dakota, Oklahoma |
| Banned for low-wage workers | Many states (thresholds vary) |
| Limited enforceability | Most other states (varies significantly) |
| FTC proposed federal ban | Proposed in 2023; status TBD as of 2025 |
| Enforceable with restrictions | Most states require reasonable scope, duration, geography |

**Best practice:** Use non-solicitation and confidentiality agreements instead of non-competes where possible. They are more enforceable and less likely to deter candidates.

### Background Check Compliance (FCRA)

The Fair Credit Reporting Act requires:
1. **Disclosure:** Provide a standalone written disclosure that a background check will be conducted
2. **Authorization:** Obtain the candidate's written consent
3. **Pre-adverse action notice:** Before taking adverse action based on the report, provide the candidate with the report and a summary of rights
4. **Adverse action notice:** After deciding not to hire based on the report, provide a final notice with the reporting agency's information and the candidate's rights

**Ban-the-box timeline:**
- Application stage: No criminal history questions
- Interview stage: Some jurisdictions allow inquiry
- Post-conditional-offer: Most jurisdictions allow background checks
- Always conduct individualized assessment (nature of offense, time elapsed, relevance to role)

## Offer Acceptance Optimization

### Why Offers Get Declined

| Reason | Frequency | Preventable? |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Better competing offer | 35% | Partially -- competitive comp and speed |
| Compensation below expectations | 25% | Yes -- calibrate expectations earlier |
| Accepted counteroffer from current employer | 15% | Partially -- address loyalty and risk |
| Negative interview experience | 10% | Yes -- interview process quality |
| Too slow (lost to faster competitor) | 10% | Yes -- reduce time-to-offer |
| Role or company concerns | 5% | Partially -- transparency throughout |

### Speed-to-Offer Benchmarks

| Metric | Good | Average | Poor |
|--------|------|---------|------|
| Interview to verbal offer | 1-3 days | 4-7 days | 8+ days |
| Verbal to written offer | Same day | 1-2 days | 3+ days |
| Total process (apply to offer) | 2-3 weeks | 4-6 weeks | 7+ weeks |
| Offer deadline | 3-5 business days | 5-7 business days | 10+ business days |

### Counteroffer Handling

When a candidate receives a counteroffer from their current employer:

1. **Acknowledge:** "I understand this is a difficult decision. Counteroffers are common."
2. **Reframe:** "Consider why you were looking in the first place. Counteroffers rarely address the root cause."
3. **Data point:** Research shows 50% of employees who accept counteroffers leave within 12 months anyway.
4. **Differentiate:** Reinforce what your opportunity offers that the current role does not (growth, mission, team, learning).
5. **Do not pressure:** "Take the time you need to make the right decision for your career."
6. **Set a deadline:** Reasonable but firm. "We would love your decision by [date] so we can move forward."

## Total Compensation Statement Template

A total compensation statement helps candidates understand the full value of an offer beyond base salary:

```
TOTAL COMPENSATION SUMMARY

                          Annual Value    4-Year Value
Base Salary               $X              $X
Signing Bonus             $X              $X (Year 1 only)
Target Annual Bonus       $X              $X
Equity (estimated)        $X/yr           $X
Benefits Value            $X              $X
  - Medical/Dental/Vision $X
  - 401(k) Match          $X
  - Life/Disability Ins.  $X
PTO Value (X days)        $X              $X
Learning Budget           $X              $X
Other Perks               $X              $X
----------------------------------------------------
Total Year 1              $X
Total Annual (ongoing)    $X
Total 4-Year Value        $X
```

This format is particularly effective when your base salary is lower than a competitor's but your total compensation is competitive or superior.
