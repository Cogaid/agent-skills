# Employee Feedback Writer - Reference Guide

## SBI Framework Deep Dive

### Origins and Research

The Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) framework was developed by the Center for Creative Leadership (CCL). Research shows that SBI-structured feedback is:

- 40% more likely to be perceived as fair by the recipient
- 60% more likely to result in behavior change
- Significantly less likely to trigger defensive reactions compared to unstructured feedback

### SBI vs. Other Feedback Models

| Model | Structure | Best For | Limitation |
|-------|----------|----------|------------|
| SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) | What happened, what you did, what resulted | All feedback types | Does not include action step |
| SBI-I (SBI + Intent) | SBI + what to do next | Constructive feedback | Can feel prescriptive |
| COIN (Context-Observation-Impact-Next) | Similar to SBI-I with explicit next steps | Performance conversations | More complex to deliver |
| EEC (Example-Effect-Change) | What happened, what it caused, what to change | Quick corrective feedback | Less structured |
| Feedforward (Goldsmith) | Focus only on future behavior, not past | Coaching and development | Ignores important past context |
| Radical Candor (Scott) | Care Personally + Challenge Directly matrix | Ongoing management | Framework, not a script |

### SBI Writing Quality Checklist

For each piece of feedback, verify:

**Situation:**
- [ ] Specific date and setting mentioned
- [ ] Context is sufficient for the reader to understand the scenario
- [ ] No evaluative language in the situation description
- [ ] The situation is relevant and recent (within the review period)

**Behavior:**
- [ ] Describes observable actions, not inferred intent
- [ ] Uses "you did" language, not "you are" language
- [ ] Avoids absolutist terms ("always," "never")
- [ ] Is specific enough that the employee will recognize the event

**Impact:**
- [ ] Connects behavior to a concrete outcome
- [ ] Describes effect on people, team, project, or business metrics
- [ ] Uses measurable terms where possible
- [ ] Is proportionate to the actual impact (no over- or under-stating)

### SBI Examples Library

**Positive Feedback Examples:**

1. Technical Excellence
   - Situation: "During the Q3 infrastructure migration project last month"
   - Behavior: "you identified a potential data loss risk in the migration script and built an automated rollback mechanism before the production cutover"
   - Impact: "This prevented what would have been a 4-hour outage affecting 50,000 users and saved the team approximately $200K in potential SLA penalties"

2. Leadership
   - Situation: "When the design team lost two members in September"
   - Behavior: "you proactively redistributed the sprint workload, held daily standups to monitor progress, and personally took on the highest-complexity tickets"
   - Impact: "The team delivered the product launch on schedule despite being understaffed, and team morale remained high throughout the crunch period"

3. Collaboration
   - Situation: "During the cross-functional budget planning sessions in October"
   - Behavior: "you prepared a data-driven comparison of vendor options and presented trade-offs clearly to both the engineering and finance teams"
   - Impact: "The group reached consensus in a single meeting rather than the usual three rounds of discussion, saving approximately 20 hours of collective meeting time"

**Constructive Feedback Examples:**

1. Communication
   - Situation: "In the last three sprint retrospectives"
   - Behavior: "you raised concerns about the deployment process but did not propose specific solutions or volunteer to lead improvements"
   - Impact: "The team perceives the concerns as complaints rather than constructive input, and the deployment process has not improved"
   - Suggested Action: "For each concern you raise, come with at least one proposed solution and an offer to help implement it"

2. Time Management
   - Situation: "Over the past quarter, in four out of six project milestones"
   - Behavior: "you delivered your component 2-3 days after the agreed deadline without flagging the delay in advance"
   - Impact: "Downstream teams had to compress their timelines, and the project was delayed by a total of 8 days"
   - Suggested Action: "When you anticipate a delay, flag it at least 3 days in advance with a revised estimate so the team can adjust"

3. Stakeholder Management
   - Situation: "In the client presentation on March 12th"
   - Behavior: "you presented the technical architecture in detail using engineering terminology without adjusting for the non-technical audience"
   - Impact: "The client asked to reschedule with a simpler overview, delaying the project approval by two weeks"
   - Suggested Action: "Before client presentations, prepare a non-technical summary and test it with a colleague outside engineering"

## Rating Calibration Framework

### Forced Distribution vs. Guided Distribution

| Approach | Description | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------------|------|------|
| Forced Distribution | Fixed percentages per rating level (e.g., 10-20-40-20-10) | Prevents rating inflation | Punishes high-performing teams; creates resentment |
| Guided Distribution | Suggested percentages with flexibility | Reduces inflation while allowing exceptions | Requires strong calibration culture |
| No Distribution | Managers rate freely | Maximum flexibility | Rating inflation inevitable without calibration |

**Recommended approach:** Guided distribution with calibration sessions. Set target ranges (not hard quotas) and require managers to justify any rating that falls outside the expected distribution.

### Calibration Session Protocol

1. **Preparation (Before Meeting)**
   - All managers submit draft reviews and ratings
   - HR compiles rating distribution by team and department
   - Identify outlier teams (all 5s, all 3s, unusual patterns)

2. **Calibration Meeting Structure (2-3 Hours)**
   - Review the rating guidelines and distribution targets (10 min)
   - Each manager presents their highest and lowest rated employees with evidence (2-3 min each)
   - Group discusses and challenges ratings, focusing on border cases (e.g., 4 vs. 5, 2 vs. 3)
   - Cross-team comparison: "Is Manager A's '5' equivalent to Manager B's '5'?"
   - Adjust ratings based on discussion

3. **Post-Calibration**
   - Managers finalize reviews incorporating calibration feedback
   - HR reviews final distribution for equity and compliance
   - Document calibration decisions for audit trail

### Common Rating Errors

| Error | Definition | Example | Fix |
|-------|-----------|---------|-----|
| Central Tendency | Rating everyone as "meets expectations" | Entire team is rated 3/5 | Require evidence for each rating; use calibration |
| Leniency Bias | Rating everyone high to avoid conflict | Average rating is 4.3/5 | Guided distribution; manager training |
| Severity Bias | Rating everyone harshly | Average rating is 2.1/5 | Cross-team calibration; benchmark examples |
| Recency Bias | Over-weighting recent events | Q4 performance dominates annual review | Require examples from each quarter; keep running notes |
| Halo Effect | One strength inflates all ratings | Star coder rated high on communication without evidence | Rate each competency independently with specific evidence |
| Horns Effect | One weakness deflates all ratings | Missed deadline leads to low ratings across all areas | Rate each competency independently with specific evidence |
| Similarity Bias | Rating people like you higher | Manager rates extroverts higher because they are extroverted | Blind calibration; diverse calibration panels |
| Contrast Effect | Rating relative to previous employee, not the rubric | Average performer rated low because they follow a superstar | Always refer back to the rubric, not other employees |

## Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

### When to Use a PIP

A Performance Improvement Plan is appropriate when:
- The employee has received clear feedback and the behavior has not changed
- The performance gap is specific, documented, and significant
- The employee has the capability to improve (it is a will/skill gap, not a role mismatch)
- The manager is genuinely committed to the employee's success (not using the PIP as a termination formality)

### PIP Structure

| Section | Content | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Performance Gap | Specific, documented deficiency with SBI examples | "In the past 6 months, 4 out of 6 deliverables missed deadline by 5+ days" |
| Expected Standard | Clear description of acceptable performance | "All deliverables submitted by agreed deadline or re-scoped 3+ days in advance" |
| Support Provided | Resources, training, and manager commitment | "Weekly progress check-ins, project management training, reduced scope during PIP" |
| Timeline | Typically 30-60-90 days | "30 days with weekly checkpoints" |
| Success Criteria | Measurable outcomes that indicate improvement | "100% of deliverables on time for the next 30 days" |
| Consequences | Clear statement of what happens if improvement is not achieved | "If expectations are not met by [date], we will proceed with [next step]" |

### PIP Best Practices

1. Never surprise an employee with a PIP. Informal feedback should precede it.
2. Involve HR from the start for legal and procedural guidance.
3. Document everything -- every conversation, every check-in, every milestone.
4. Provide genuine support. A PIP should be a real chance to improve, not a paper trail for termination.
5. Set realistic timelines. 30 days is appropriate for behavioral issues; 60-90 days for skill development.
6. Check in frequently (weekly minimum) and document progress.
7. Be prepared for either outcome -- success or separation.

## 360 Feedback Administration

### When to Use 360 Feedback

| Use Case | Appropriate | Not Appropriate |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Leadership development | Yes | |
| Self-awareness building | Yes | |
| Performance evaluation input | Carefully, as one data point | As the sole basis for rating |
| Promotion decisions | Yes, as supporting evidence | As the primary decision factor |
| Team dynamics assessment | Yes | |
| Compensation decisions | No | Distorts honesty of feedback |
| Termination decisions | No | Legal and fairness risks |

### Reviewer Selection Guidelines

| Reviewer Type | Number | Selection Criteria |
|--------------|--------|-------------------|
| Direct manager | 1 | Mandatory |
| Peers | 2-4 | Regular collaborators, diverse perspectives |
| Direct reports | 2-4 (if applicable) | Aim for full team if team is small |
| Cross-functional partners | 1-3 | Regular collaborators from other teams |
| Skip-level manager | 0-1 | Optional, for senior roles |
| Self | 1 | Always include self-assessment |

**Total reviewers:** 6-12 is the sweet spot. Fewer than 5 reduces reliability; more than 12 creates survey fatigue.

### Anonymity and Confidentiality

- **Individual feedback from peers and direct reports should be anonymous.** This ensures candor.
- **Manager feedback is not anonymous.** Managers should own their feedback.
- **Minimum threshold for aggregation:** Show verbatim comments only when there are 3+ respondents in a category (to prevent identification).
- **Never share raw data.** Aggregate and summarize themes.

## Difficult Conversations Framework

### The Radical Candor Matrix (Kim Scott)

| | Challenge Directly (High) | Challenge Directly (Low) |
|---|---|---|
| **Care Personally (High)** | Radical Candor (ideal) | Ruinous Empathy (kind but unclear) |
| **Care Personally (Low)** | Obnoxious Aggression (harsh and clear) | Manipulative Insincerity (neither caring nor clear) |

Goal: Operate in the Radical Candor quadrant -- care enough to be honest, and be honest enough to show you care.

### Emotional Response Preparation

| Employee Reaction | Your Response | Key Phrases |
|------------------|--------------|-------------|
| Tears | Pause, offer tissue, show empathy, continue when ready | "I can see this is emotional. Take your time. This conversation comes from wanting to help." |
| Anger | Stay calm, do not escalate, acknowledge the emotion | "I understand you are frustrated. I hear you. Let us work through this together." |
| Denial | Provide specific evidence, do not argue about feelings | "I appreciate your perspective. Here are the specific examples I observed..." |
| Deflection to others | Redirect gently to the individual | "I understand there are team dynamics at play. Right now, I want to focus on what you can control." |
| Shutdown / silence | Give space, offer to reconvene | "I know this is a lot. Would it help to take some time and continue this conversation tomorrow?" |
| Agreement / over-compliance | Verify understanding, ensure genuine commitment | "I appreciate your openness. Can you tell me in your own words what you are going to do differently?" |
