# Employee Feedback Writer

Craft clear, actionable, and balanced performance feedback using the SBI framework, structured review templates, and growth planning tools.

## Purpose

Performance feedback is the most impactful management tool for employee development, yet most managers struggle to write it well. Vague feedback ("great job") fails to reinforce behavior. Harsh feedback without structure damages trust. This skill provides frameworks and templates for writing feedback that is specific, fair, growth-oriented, and legally defensible across all review contexts.

## Quick Reference

### SBI Framework (Situation-Behavior-Impact)

The SBI model is the gold standard for delivering specific, objective feedback.

| Component | What It Covers | Example (Positive) | Example (Constructive) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **S**ituation | When and where the behavior occurred | "During last Tuesday's client presentation..." | "In the sprint planning meeting on March 5th..." |
| **B**ehavior | Observable action (no interpretation) | "...you anticipated the client's objections and addressed each one with data." | "...you interrupted two colleagues mid-sentence and dismissed their estimates without discussion." |
| **I**mpact | The effect on people, team, or outcomes | "The client signed the contract that day, and the team felt confident in our preparation." | "The team members disengaged for the rest of the meeting, and we left without alignment on the sprint scope." |

### Feedback Types

| Type | When to Use | Tone | Frequency |
|------|------------|------|-----------|
| Positive / Reinforcing | When you want behavior repeated | Celebratory, specific | Immediately + in writing |
| Constructive / Corrective | When behavior needs to change | Direct, respectful, solution-focused | Promptly, in private |
| Developmental / Coaching | When growing capability over time | Curious, forward-looking | In 1:1s, quarterly |
| Evaluative / Formal | Periodic performance assessment | Balanced, evidence-based | Quarterly or annually |
| Peer / 360 | Multi-source perspective | Collegial, observation-based | Semi-annually or annually |

### Rating Calibration Guide

| Rating | Label | Definition | Distribution Target |
|--------|-------|------------|-------------------|
| 5 | Exceptional | Consistently exceeds all expectations; top 5-10% performer | 5-10% |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Frequently exceeds expectations in key areas | 20-25% |
| 3 | Meets Expectations | Consistently delivers on all role requirements | 40-50% |
| 2 | Partially Meets | Meets some expectations but has significant gaps | 15-20% |
| 1 | Does Not Meet | Falls below expectations in most areas | 5-10% |

### Calibration Questions

Before assigning a rating, ask yourself:

- Would I give this same rating to another employee with identical performance?
- Can I cite 3+ specific examples supporting this rating?
- Am I rating recent performance or the full review period (recency bias)?
- Am I rating the person or their work output (halo/horns effect)?
- Have I accounted for factors outside the employee's control?

## Difficult Conversation Guide

### Preparing for Difficult Feedback

| Step | Action | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Gather specific examples (3+ incidents) | Build evidence-based case |
| 2 | Separate behavior from intent | Avoid character judgments |
| 3 | Identify the business impact | Connect feedback to outcomes |
| 4 | Define the desired change clearly | Give actionable direction |
| 5 | Anticipate reactions (denial, deflection, emotion) | Prepare responses |
| 6 | Choose a private, neutral setting | Create psychological safety |
| 7 | Plan follow-up and support | Show commitment to improvement |

### Difficult Feedback Script

```
Opening:
"Thank you for making time for this conversation. I want to discuss
[topic] because I care about your success and want to make sure we
are aligned on expectations."

SBI Delivery:
"I have noticed [specific behavior] in [specific situations]. The
impact has been [concrete effect on team/project/outcomes]."

Bridge:
"I am sharing this because [positive intent: I want you to succeed /
this is important for the team / I know you can do better]."

Expectation:
"Going forward, I need to see [specific changed behavior]. What I
am looking for is [clear description of expected behavior]."

Support:
"I want to help you succeed here. What support would be helpful?
[Offer specific resources: training, coaching, adjusted workload]."

Agreement:
"Let us agree on [specific next steps and timeline]. I would like
to check in on this in [timeframe]. Does that work for you?"

Close:
"I appreciate you hearing this. I am confident we can work through
this together."
```

### Common Reactions and Responses

| Reaction | Response Strategy |
|----------|------------------|
| Denial ("That did not happen") | "I understand that is not how you remember it. Here are the specific instances I observed..." |
| Deflection ("Everyone does that") | "I appreciate the context, but I am focused on your specific behavior and how we can improve it." |
| Emotion (tears, anger) | "I can see this is difficult. Would you like a moment? This conversation comes from a place of wanting to help." |
| Counter-attack ("You never...") | "I want to hear your perspective, and we can discuss that separately. Right now, let us focus on [the topic]." |
| Shutdown (silence) | "I understand this is a lot to process. Take some time and let us continue this conversation [tomorrow/next week]." |

## Workflow

### Writing a Performance Review

1. **Gather Data (1-2 weeks before)**
   - [ ] Review goal tracking and OKR progress
   - [ ] Read self-assessment from employee
   - [ ] Review 360 feedback (if applicable)
   - [ ] Review 1:1 notes from the period
   - [ ] Check project outcomes and metrics
   - [ ] Note specific examples for each competency

2. **Draft the Review**
   - [ ] Rate each competency with evidence
   - [ ] Write 2-3 key accomplishments using SBI format
   - [ ] Write 1-2 development areas using SBI format
   - [ ] Draft overall summary balancing strengths and growth areas
   - [ ] Set goals for next period
   - [ ] Determine compensation recommendation (if applicable)

3. **Calibrate**
   - [ ] Compare ratings across your team for consistency
   - [ ] Participate in calibration sessions with peer managers
   - [ ] Adjust ratings based on calibration feedback
   - [ ] Verify rating distribution aligns with guidelines

4. **Deliver**
   - [ ] Schedule dedicated 45-60 minute meeting
   - [ ] Share written review 24 hours before the meeting (optional, org-dependent)
   - [ ] Present feedback conversationally, not as a reading exercise
   - [ ] Listen to employee response and discuss
   - [ ] Agree on goals and development actions
   - [ ] Document any additional notes from the discussion

5. **Follow Up**
   - [ ] File completed review in HRIS
   - [ ] Begin tracking new goals
   - [ ] Schedule growth plan check-ins
   - [ ] Process compensation changes

## Scripts & Tools

### Usage: Generate Performance Review

```
Input: Employee role, level, goals, accomplishments, development areas, self-assessment
Process: Select template (quarterly/annual) -> Structure feedback using SBI -> Calibrate rating -> Draft growth plan
Output: Complete review document ready for calibration and delivery
```

### Usage: Write Feedback for a Specific Situation

```
Input: Description of the situation and behavior
Process: Apply SBI framework -> Determine feedback type -> Draft talking points -> Suggest follow-up actions
Output: Structured feedback with delivery script
```

### Usage: Build a Growth Plan

```
Input: Current role/level, target role/level, identified gaps, available resources
Process: Map skill gaps -> Identify development actions -> Create timeline -> Define success metrics
Output: Complete individual growth plan with check-in schedule
```

## Best Practices

1. **Be specific, always.** "You did great on the project" is not feedback. "Your analysis of the churn data identified two retention strategies that reduced churn by 15%" is feedback.
2. **Separate observation from interpretation.** Describe what you saw, not what you think it means.
3. **Balance positive and constructive feedback throughout the year.** Reviews should never contain surprises.
4. **Give feedback in real time.** The closer to the event, the more impactful the feedback. Do not save it for the annual review.
5. **Make it a conversation, not a monologue.** Ask for the employee's perspective and listen.
6. **Focus on behavior, not personality.** "You were disorganized" attacks character. "The project plan was missing deadlines and dependencies" describes fixable behavior.
7. **Tie development areas to growth opportunities.** "Here is what to improve" is less motivating than "Here is what unlocks your next level."
8. **Document consistently.** Maintain a running log of examples throughout the review period to avoid recency bias.
9. **Calibrate with peers.** Your "meets expectations" should mean the same as another manager's.
10. **Follow through.** Feedback without follow-up is noise. Check in on development actions regularly.
