# Interview Question Generator

Generate calibrated, role-specific interview questions across behavioral, technical, situational, and culture-add dimensions with scoring rubrics and evaluation templates.

## Purpose

Great interviews are designed, not improvised. This skill produces structured interview questions tied to specific competencies, calibrated by role level, and paired with evaluation criteria. Structured interviews are 2x more predictive of job performance than unstructured ones and significantly reduce bias in hiring decisions.

## Quick Reference

### Question Type Overview

| Type | Purpose | When to Use | Example Stem |
|------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Behavioral | Assess past behavior as predictor of future behavior | All rounds | "Tell me about a time when..." |
| Technical | Evaluate domain-specific knowledge and problem-solving | Technical screens, on-sites | "How would you design/build/debug..." |
| Situational | Test judgment in hypothetical scenarios | Mid-to-senior roles | "What would you do if..." |
| Culture-Add | Assess values alignment and unique perspective | Final rounds | "What matters most to you in..." |
| Case Study | Evaluate analytical thinking and structured problem-solving | Strategy, consulting, PM roles | "Walk me through how you would approach..." |
| Role Play | Test real-time interpersonal skills | Sales, support, management roles | "Let us simulate a scenario where..." |

### STAR Framework for Evaluation

Use STAR to evaluate behavioral responses consistently.

| Component | What to Listen For | Strong Signal | Weak Signal |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|-------------|
| **S**ituation | Specific context and setting | Clear, relevant scenario with stakes | Vague or generic setup |
| **T**ask | The candidate's specific responsibility | Distinct personal ownership | Team effort without individual role |
| **A**ction | Concrete steps the candidate took | Detailed, logical sequence of actions | Abstract or hypothetical actions |
| **R**esult | Measurable outcome and learning | Quantified impact + reflection | No outcome stated or only positive spin |

### Difficulty Calibration by Level

| Level | Question Depth | Scope | Expected Evidence |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------------------|
| Junior / Entry | Foundational concepts, learning examples | Individual tasks | Academic projects, internships, early career |
| Mid-Level | Applied expertise, independent problem-solving | Project-level | 2-5 years of professional examples |
| Senior | Strategic thinking, ambiguity, mentorship | Team / system-level | Cross-functional leadership, architecture decisions |
| Lead / Principal | Organizational influence, trade-off reasoning | Department / company-level | Business impact, team building, technical vision |
| Executive | Vision, culture, P&L, stakeholder management | Company / market-level | Transformation stories, board-level decisions |

## Question Banks by Competency

### Problem Solving

| Level | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Junior | "Describe a challenging assignment from school or an internship. How did you break it down and work through it?" |
| Mid | "Tell me about a time you solved a problem that had no obvious solution. What was your process?" |
| Senior | "Describe a situation where you had to make a critical decision with incomplete information. What framework did you use?" |
| Exec | "Tell me about a time you identified a systemic problem in an organization and drove a solution across multiple teams." |

### Collaboration and Communication

| Level | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Junior | "Describe a group project where you had to work with people who had different working styles." |
| Mid | "Tell me about a time you had to influence someone without direct authority to get a project moving." |
| Senior | "Give an example of a cross-functional initiative you led. How did you align stakeholders with competing priorities?" |
| Exec | "Describe how you built alignment across an executive team on a controversial strategic decision." |

### Leadership and Ownership

| Level | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Junior | "Tell me about a time you took initiative on something beyond your assigned responsibilities." |
| Mid | "Describe a project you owned end-to-end. What went well and what would you do differently?" |
| Senior | "Tell me about a time you mentored someone through a significant challenge. What was your approach and what was the outcome?" |
| Exec | "How have you built and scaled a high-performing team? Walk me through your philosophy and a concrete example." |

### Adaptability and Resilience

| Level | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Junior | "Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?" |
| Mid | "Describe a situation where project requirements changed significantly midstream. How did you handle it?" |
| Senior | "Tell me about a time you had to pivot a team's direction due to a major change in strategy or market conditions." |
| Exec | "Describe a significant organizational change you led. How did you manage resistance and maintain team performance?" |

### Technical Depth (Customize per Role)

| Category | Sample Question |
|----------|----------------|
| System Design | "Design a [relevant system] that handles [scale requirement]. Walk me through your architecture decisions." |
| Debugging | "You receive a report that [system] is [failing in specific way]. Walk me through your investigation process." |

## Illegal and Inappropriate Questions to Avoid

These questions are illegal or high-risk in most jurisdictions. Never ask them.

| Category | Do NOT Ask | Ask Instead |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Age | "When did you graduate?" / "How old are you?" | "Do you meet the minimum age requirement for this role?" |
| Race / Ethnicity | "Where are you originally from?" | "Are you authorized to work in [country]?" |
| Religion | "Do you observe any religious holidays?" | "This role requires [specific schedule]. Can you meet that?" |
| Family Status | "Do you have children?" / "Are you planning to start a family?" | "This role requires [travel/hours]. Are you able to meet those requirements?" |
| Disability | "Do you have any disabilities?" | "Can you perform the essential functions of this role with or without accommodation?" |
| Gender / Orientation | "Are you married?" / Any gender or orientation question | Do not ask; not relevant to job performance |
| Pregnancy | Any question about pregnancy or family planning | Do not ask; illegal in most jurisdictions |
| Arrest Record | "Have you ever been arrested?" | "Have you been convicted of a crime relevant to this role?" (where legally permitted) |
| Salary History | "What did you make at your last job?" (illegal in many jurisdictions) | "What are your compensation expectations for this role?" |

### Situational Question Builder Template

```
Context: "Imagine you are in your first [timeframe] in this role..."
Scenario: "[Realistic challenge the role will actually face]"
Constraint: "[Real limitation: budget, timeline, team size, politics]"
Question: "How would you approach this? Walk me through your thinking."

Evaluation Criteria:
- Did they ask clarifying questions? (judgment)
- Did they structure their approach? (problem-solving)
- Did they consider stakeholders? (collaboration)
- Did they acknowledge trade-offs? (maturity)
- Was the solution realistic? (practical experience)
```

## Workflow

### Step 1: Define Interview Objectives

- [ ] Review the job description and screening criteria
- [ ] Identify 4-6 key competencies to evaluate
- [ ] Assign competencies to interview rounds (no duplication)
- [ ] Determine the question types needed per round

### Step 2: Generate Questions

- [ ] Select 3-5 questions per competency from the question bank
- [ ] Calibrate difficulty to the role level
- [ ] Write follow-up probes for each question
- [ ] Review questions against the illegal questions list
- [ ] Add role-specific technical questions

### Step 3: Build Evaluation Materials

- [ ] Create scorecards for each interviewer
- [ ] Define what "good" looks like for each question (answer rubric)
- [ ] Prepare the interview plan with timing and logistics
- [ ] Brief interviewers on their focus areas and questions

### Step 4: Post-Interview

- [ ] Collect scorecards within 24 hours
- [ ] Run structured debrief using scores, not gut feel
- [ ] Document decision rationale
- [ ] Provide candidate feedback if requested


## Best Practices

1. **Ask every candidate the same core questions.** Structured interviews are more predictive and more defensible.
2. **Separate evaluation from data gathering.** Take notes during the interview; score after.
3. **Use follow-up probes, not leading questions.** "Tell me more about that" beats "So you must have felt frustrated?"
4. **Evaluate independently before the debrief.** Submit scorecards before discussing with other interviewers to avoid groupthink.
5. **Calibrate across interviewers.** Ensure everyone scores "3" the same way by reviewing example answers together.
6. **Balance question types.** No interview should be 100% behavioral or 100% technical.
7. **Leave time for candidate questions.** How candidates ask questions reveals priorities, judgment, and preparation.
8. **Rotate questions periodically.** If candidates share questions online, refresh the bank quarterly.
9. **Never ask about protected characteristics.** When in doubt, ask: "Does this question predict job performance?" If not, cut it.
10. **Time-box each question.** Allocate 8-12 minutes per behavioral question to allow depth without running over.
