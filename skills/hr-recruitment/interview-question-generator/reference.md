# Interview Question Generator - Reference Guide

## Structured Interview Research

### Evidence Base

Structured interviews are significantly more predictive of job performance than unstructured ones:

| Interview Type | Validity Coefficient | Source |
|---------------|---------------------|--------|
| Unstructured Interview | 0.20 | Schmidt & Hunter (1998) |
| Structured Interview | 0.51 | Schmidt & Hunter (1998) |
| Work Sample Test | 0.54 | Schmidt & Hunter (1998) |
| GMA (Cognitive Ability) | 0.51 | Schmidt & Hunter (1998) |
| Structured + GMA combined | 0.63 | Schmidt & Hunter (1998) |

A validity coefficient of 0.51 means structured interviews explain approximately 26% of the variance in job performance -- far better than the 4% explained by unstructured interviews.

### What Makes an Interview "Structured"

| Element | Description | Impact |
|---------|------------|--------|
| Standardized questions | Same core questions for every candidate | Enables fair comparison |
| Scoring rubric | Predefined criteria for each score level | Reduces subjective evaluation |
| Interviewer training | Calibration on scoring and bias awareness | Improves inter-rater reliability |
| Question job-relatedness | Every question tied to a job competency | Legal defensibility |
| Consistent administration | Same order, timing, and format | Reduces variability |
| Independent scoring | Interviewers score before comparing notes | Prevents groupthink |
| Panel interviews | Multiple interviewers per session | Reduces individual bias |

## STAR Framework Deep Dive

### Advanced STAR Evaluation

Beyond the basic STAR framework, experienced interviewers listen for these advanced signals:

**Situation Quality Indicators:**
- Specificity: Exact dates, team sizes, project names
- Stakes: Why the situation mattered, what was at risk
- Relevance: How closely the situation maps to the target role
- Complexity: Degree of ambiguity, competing priorities, constraints

**Task Quality Indicators:**
- Ownership: "I" vs. "we" -- the candidate's specific responsibility
- Scope: Was the task significant or trivial?
- Initiative: Was the task assigned or self-identified?
- Clarity: Did the candidate understand what success looked like?

**Action Quality Indicators:**
- Sequence: Logical progression of steps
- Decision-making: Why they chose this approach over alternatives
- Collaboration: How they worked with others
- Adaptation: How they adjusted when things changed
- Skills demonstration: Evidence of the competency being evaluated

**Result Quality Indicators:**
- Quantification: Numbers, percentages, dollar amounts
- Attribution: Clearly the candidate's contribution, not the team's
- Learning: What they would do differently
- Durability: Did the result persist? Was it a lasting impact?
- Scope of impact: Individual, team, department, or company level

### Common STAR Pitfalls

| Pitfall | How to Detect | Follow-up Probe |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| Hypothetical response | "I would..." instead of "I did..." | "Can you give me a specific example of when you actually did this?" |
| Team answer without individual role | "We decided to..." with no personal contribution | "What was your specific role in that decision?" |
| Result without metrics | "It went well" or "They were happy" | "How did you measure the outcome? What were the numbers?" |
| Rehearsed/generic answer | Too polished, lacks specificity | "Tell me about a time it did not go as well" or "Walk me through the messiest part" |
| Too-old example | Example from 10+ years ago for a current skill | "Do you have a more recent example?" |
| Only positive examples | No evidence of learning from failure | "Tell me about a time this approach did not work" |

## Competency Framework Reference

### Universal Competencies (All Roles)

| Competency | Definition | Observable Behaviors |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Communication | Conveying information clearly and effectively | Adjusts style to audience; listens actively; writes clearly; presents confidently |
| Collaboration | Working effectively with others toward shared goals | Shares credit; seeks input; resolves conflict constructively; supports teammates |
| Problem Solving | Identifying issues and developing effective solutions | Breaks down complexity; considers multiple approaches; uses data; tests assumptions |
| Adaptability | Adjusting to change and ambiguity | Stays effective under uncertainty; pivots when needed; learns from new situations |
| Ownership | Taking responsibility and following through | Meets commitments; escalates appropriately; does not blame others; sees tasks to completion |
| Growth Mindset | Seeking feedback and continuous improvement | Solicits feedback; acknowledges gaps; invests in learning; applies lessons |

### Leadership Competencies (Manager+ Roles)

| Competency | Definition | Observable Behaviors |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| People Development | Growing the capabilities of team members | Coaches regularly; creates growth opportunities; gives actionable feedback; supports career goals |
| Strategic Thinking | Connecting work to broader organizational goals | Prioritizes effectively; anticipates consequences; balances short and long-term; sees systems |
| Influence | Persuading and aligning others without authority | Builds coalitions; communicates vision; negotiates effectively; earns trust |
| Decision Making | Making timely, well-reasoned choices | Gathers relevant data; weighs trade-offs; commits to decisions; course-corrects when wrong |
| Organizational Awareness | Understanding how the organization works | Navigates politics constructively; understands power dynamics; builds cross-functional relationships |

### Technical Competencies (Engineering Roles)

| Competency | Junior Signal | Mid Signal | Senior Signal | Staff+ Signal |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| System Design | Can explain basic architectures | Designs components within a system | Designs end-to-end systems with trade-off analysis | Defines architecture vision across multiple systems |
| Code Quality | Writes readable code | Writes maintainable, tested code | Sets quality standards for the team | Defines engineering practices org-wide |
| Debugging | Finds bugs with guidance | Debugs independently using systematic approaches | Debugs complex distributed systems | Identifies and resolves systemic issues |
| Technical Communication | Documents own work | Writes design docs for team consumption | Creates technical strategy documents | Communicates technical vision to executives |

## Interview Anti-Patterns

### Questions That Do Not Predict Performance

| Question | Why It Fails | Better Alternative |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| "What is your greatest weakness?" | Rewards rehearsed answers, not self-awareness | "Tell me about a skill you have actively worked to improve in the last year. What was your approach?" |
| "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" | Rewards ambition performance, not actual planning | "What are you hoping to learn or achieve in your next role?" |
| "Why should we hire you?" | Rewards sales ability, not job fit | "What about this role excites you most, and what do you think you would find challenging?" |
| Brain teasers / puzzles | Tests puzzle-solving, not job skills; advantages certain educational backgrounds | Role-relevant problem-solving questions |
| "How many golf balls fit in a school bus?" | Zero correlation with job performance (Google abandoned these) | Case study based on actual work scenarios |
| Stress interviews | Measures stress response, not competence; damages employer brand | Realistic challenges with appropriate support |

### Interview Process Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----|
| Marathon interviews (6+ hours) | Candidate fatigue degrades signal quality | Cap at 4 hours with breaks |
| Redundant questions across rounds | Wastes time; frustrates candidates | Map competencies to rounds with no overlap |
| No debrief structure | Loudest voice wins; recency bias dominates | Score independently, then structured debrief |
| Immediate hire/no-hire gut call | Anchoring on first impression | Require scored evidence for every recommendation |
| Testing for knowledge vs. ability | Memorization is not competence | Ask candidates to apply knowledge to scenarios |
| No candidate questions period | Misses signal on priorities and judgment | Reserve 10+ minutes for candidate questions |

## Follow-Up Probe Library

### Universal Probes (Work with Any Behavioral Question)

| Probe | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| "What was your specific role in that?" | Clarify individual vs. team contribution |
| "What alternatives did you consider?" | Assess decision-making breadth |
| "What would you do differently if you faced this again?" | Test self-awareness and learning |
| "How did you measure the outcome?" | Push for quantified results |
| "What was the most difficult part?" | Reveal honest assessment and resilience |
| "How did others react to your approach?" | Assess interpersonal awareness |
| "Walk me through your thought process step by step." | Reveal problem-solving methodology |
| "What did you learn from this experience?" | Test growth orientation |
| "How did the context or constraints influence your approach?" | Assess situational judgment |
| "What feedback did you receive, and did you agree with it?" | Test receptivity to feedback |

### Technical Probes

| Probe | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| "What trade-offs did you consider in that design?" | Assess engineering judgment |
| "How would this scale to 10x / 100x?" | Test scalability thinking |
| "What would break first under load?" | Assess failure mode analysis |
| "How would you test this?" | Evaluate quality orientation |
| "How would you explain this to a non-technical stakeholder?" | Test communication skills |
| "What would you do differently with unlimited time/resources?" | Understand ideal vs. pragmatic thinking |

## Legal Reference by Country

### United States
- Title VII of the Civil Rights Act: No discrimination based on protected classes
- ADA: Accommodate disabilities; ask only about ability to perform essential functions
- ADEA: No age-based discrimination (40+)
- Salary history bans: 20+ states and cities

### European Union (GDPR implications)
- Data minimization: Only collect information necessary for the hiring decision
- Purpose limitation: Interview data used only for the stated hiring purpose
- Right to erasure: Candidates may request deletion of their interview data
- Consent: Must have explicit consent to record interviews

### United Kingdom
- Equality Act 2010: Nine protected characteristics
- Right to Work checks: Must verify but cannot discriminate based on nationality
- Rehabilitation of Offenders Act: Spent convictions generally not disclosable

### Canada
- Canadian Human Rights Act: 13 prohibited grounds of discrimination
- Provincial human rights codes vary
- Pay equity legislation in several provinces
