# Resume Screener

Systematically evaluate resumes against job criteria using structured rubrics, bias-aware checklists, and consistent scoring to build a qualified, diverse shortlist.

## Purpose

Resume screening is the highest-volume activity in recruiting and the most prone to inconsistency and bias. This skill provides a repeatable framework that ensures every candidate is evaluated against the same criteria, reduces unconscious bias, and produces defensible hiring decisions with clear documentation.

## Quick Reference

### Screening Criteria Matrix

Build this matrix before reviewing any resumes. Every criterion must trace back to the job description.

| Category | Criterion | Weight | Must-Have | Scoring (0-3) |
|----------|-----------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Education | Relevant degree or equivalent experience | 10% | No | 0=None, 1=Related field, 2=Target field, 3=Advanced degree |
| Experience | Years in relevant role | 20% | Yes | 0=None, 1=<50% required, 2=Meets requirement, 3=Exceeds |
| Technical Skills | Core skill proficiency | 25% | Yes | 0=Not mentioned, 1=Basic, 2=Proficient, 3=Expert with examples |
| Domain Knowledge | Industry/sector experience | 15% | No | 0=None, 1=Adjacent, 2=Direct, 3=Deep with outcomes |
| Achievements | Measurable impact demonstrated | 15% | No | 0=None, 1=Vague claims, 2=Some metrics, 3=Clear quantified results |
| Culture Alignment | Values, work style, mission fit | 10% | No | 0=No signal, 1=Weak signal, 2=Moderate, 3=Strong evidence |
| Communication | Resume quality, clarity, structure | 5% | No | 0=Poor, 1=Adequate, 2=Good, 3=Excellent |

### Scoring Rubric

| Score | Label | Definition |
|-------|-------|------------|
| 0 | Not Evident | No evidence of the criterion on the resume |
| 1 | Below Threshold | Some relevance but does not meet minimum bar |
| 2 | Meets Threshold | Clearly meets the stated requirement |
| 3 | Exceeds | Strong evidence with demonstrated outcomes beyond the requirement |

### Decision Thresholds

| Weighted Score | Decision | Action |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| 80-100% | Advance | Move to phone screen immediately |
| 60-79% | Hold | Review with hiring manager for borderline decision |
| 40-59% | Waitlist | Park for potential future consideration |
| 0-39% | Decline | Send respectful rejection |

## Red and Green Flags

### Green Flags (Positive Indicators)

| Flag | Why It Matters |
|------|---------------|
| Quantified achievements ("increased revenue 40%") | Demonstrates impact orientation |
| Progressive responsibility / promotions | Shows growth trajectory |
| Relevant side projects or open-source contributions | Signals genuine interest beyond employment |
| Tailored resume matching the JD | Indicates genuine interest and attention to detail |
| Consistent tenure (2+ years per role) | Suggests stability and ability to see projects through |
| Recommendations or endorsements cited | External validation of capabilities |
| Clear narrative connecting past roles | Shows intentional career development |
| Industry certifications relevant to role | Investment in professional development |

### Red Flags (Investigate, Do Not Auto-Reject)

| Flag | What to Investigate | Bias Risk |
|------|-------------------|-----------|
| Employment gaps | May indicate caregiving, health, education, or career pivot | Gender and disability bias |
| Frequent job changes (<1 year) | Could be contract work, layoffs, or startup environment | Industry and age bias |
| Overqualified on paper | May be career-shifting or seeking work-life balance | Age bias |
| Non-traditional education | Bootcamps, self-taught, alternative credentials are increasingly valid | Socioeconomic bias |
| Foreign experience / credentials | Equivalent qualifications may use different terminology | National origin bias |
| Older graduation dates | Experience may be highly relevant regardless of when obtained | Age bias |
| Non-linear career path | May bring diverse perspective and transferable skills | Conformity bias |

## Bias-Awareness Checklist

Complete this checklist before and during every screening session.

### Pre-Screening

- [ ] I have reviewed the job description and confirmed the criteria matrix
- [ ] I have removed or hidden candidate names, photos, and demographic identifiers (if blind screening)
- [ ] I am screening all resumes in the same session or using the same rubric across sessions
- [ ] I have acknowledged my own potential biases (affinity, halo, confirmation, anchoring)
- [ ] I am prepared to score each criterion independently, not holistically

### During Screening

- [ ] I am evaluating skills and experience, not pedigree (school name, company name)
- [ ] I am giving equal weight to equivalent experience regardless of where it was gained
- [ ] I am not penalizing employment gaps without investigation
- [ ] I am considering transferable skills from adjacent industries
- [ ] I am not confusing resume formatting quality with candidate quality
- [ ] I am not anchoring on the first few resumes I reviewed

### Post-Screening

- [ ] My shortlist includes candidates from diverse backgrounds
- [ ] I can articulate a specific, criteria-based reason for every rejection
- [ ] I have documented scores for auditability
- [ ] I have flagged any borderline candidates for hiring manager review

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Setup (Before Any Resumes Arrive)

1. Build the screening criteria matrix from the approved job description
2. Assign weights to each criterion with the hiring manager
3. Define must-have vs. nice-to-have requirements
4. Set decision thresholds (advance / hold / decline percentages)
5. Decide on blind screening protocol (names, photos, schools)
6. Prepare the screening scorecard template

### Phase 2: First Pass (2-3 Minutes Per Resume)

1. Check for must-have requirements only
2. Score each must-have criterion (0-3)
3. If any must-have scores 0, move to decline pile with documented reason
4. If all must-haves score 1+, move to detailed review pile

### Phase 3: Detailed Review (5-7 Minutes Per Resume)

1. Score all criteria on the matrix
2. Calculate weighted total score
3. Note specific evidence for each score in the comments column
4. Flag any red flags for investigation
5. Assign to decision bucket (advance / hold / waitlist / decline)

### Phase 4: Calibration

1. Review the distribution of scores across all candidates
2. Compare borderline cases side-by-side using criteria scores only
3. Discuss holds with hiring manager using documented evidence
4. Finalize the shortlist

### Phase 5: Documentation and Communication

1. Record all decisions in the ATS
2. Send advancement notifications to shortlisted candidates
3. Send respectful rejections to declined candidates
4. Archive screening scorecards for compliance

## ATS Keyword Matching Guidance

### How ATS Keyword Matching Works

| ATS Feature | What It Does | Implication for Screeners |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Keyword match | Scans for exact terms from the JD | Candidates may use synonyms that ATS misses |
| Boolean search | AND/OR/NOT logic for filtering | Overly strict Boolean filters eliminate good candidates |
| Semantic matching | Advanced ATS matches related terms | Less common; do not rely on it |
| Section parsing | Extracts work history, education, skills | Non-standard resume formats may parse incorrectly |

### Compensating for ATS Limitations

1. **Review ATS rejects manually** (sample 10-15%) to catch false negatives
2. **Use synonym lists** when setting up keyword filters (e.g., "PM" = "Product Manager" = "Program Manager")
3. **Do not over-filter.** Fewer filters produce a larger but higher-quality pool
4. **Check parsed data.** If the ATS parsed incorrectly, review the original document
5. **Weight keyword density appropriately.** A candidate who mentions a skill once in context may be stronger than one who keyword-stuffs

```

```

```
Output: Consistency report with calibration recommendations
```

## Best Practices

1. **Never screen when fatigued.** Decision quality drops after 25-30 resumes. Take breaks.
2. **Randomize resume order.** Avoid anchoring bias from the first few candidates reviewed.
3. **Score criteria independently.** Do not let a strong score in one area inflate others (halo effect).
4. **Document everything.** Every decision should have a traceable, criteria-based rationale.
5. **Sample-check your rejects.** Periodically review declined candidates to catch systematic errors.
6. **Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves ruthlessly.** If it is not truly required, it should not eliminate candidates.
7. **Calibrate with the hiring manager early.** Screen 5-10 resumes together before screening independently.
8. **Track pipeline diversity.** Monitor demographic representation at each stage to identify drop-off points.
